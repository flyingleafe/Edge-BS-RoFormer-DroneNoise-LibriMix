# Narrative — Recovering microphone-array geometry from audio
kind: report
audience: A reader with shallow context on this project but solid general signal-processing, acoustics, and multi-mic-array background. They know what a steering vector, cross-spectrum, and TDOA are; they do NOT know this project's datasets or code. No prior artifact assumed.
through-line: A physics-based rotor-noise model needs the true positions of every microphone and rotor. We show that the geometry accompanying two drone-array datasets is silently wrong — a 180° frame mismatch in one, a wrong array plane in the other — and, crucially, that whether you can *fix it from the audio* depends on the recording geometry. For DREGON (clean single-rotor recordings, rotors surrounding the array) audio-only self-calibration is well-posed and recovers the geometry. For Michael's (rotors clustered below the array, noisy flight harmonics) the same problem is degenerate — an honest negative result we make legible with a rigid-ring model — so the fix comes from the rig photo instead. The punchline: audio can self-calibrate a mic array, but only when the source geometry makes it observable; know which case you are in.

## Sections (ordered)
1. Why positions matter, and why we doubted them — message: a propagation model turns geometry into predicted inter-mic delays and levels, so a wrong position silently corrupts the model — and we saw the symptom (a missing mid-band). — evidence: schematic of 1/r+delay propagation; the phase-error-vs-frequency argument (15-sample delay error = 12° at 100 Hz but 120° at 1 kHz → mid-band cancels). — sources: notebooks/stage0_rtf_utils.py (freefield_rtf, freefield_tdoa_row), src/models/generative/positional_harmonic_gen.py
2. Reading geometry off the audio: TDOA and the RTF — message: with a single source recorded on all mics, GCC-PHAT gives the true inter-mic delays and the relative transfer function (RTF) gives per-mic magnitude+phase, with coherence telling us which frequencies to trust. — evidence: RTF/coherence figure (measured vs free-field, per mic) from Stage-0; the 400–800 Hz "clean band" (below = wind, above = decorrelation, γ²≈0.5–0.7). — sources: notebooks/stage0_rtf_utils.py (gcc_phat_tdoa, estimate_rtf), notebooks/stage0_rotor_rtf.ipynb
3. Discovery: a 180° frame mismatch (DREGON) — message: the measured TDOAs are anti-correlated (−0.55) with what the shipped geometry predicts; rotating the mic frame ~183° about z restores agreement to +0.93 — the shipped micPos/rotorsPos live in mismatched frames. — evidence: frame-alignment sweep (correlation vs rotation angle) + measured-vs-predicted TDOA scatter (shipped vs corrected). — sources: notebooks/stage0_rtf_utils.py (align_mic_frame), notebooks/stage0_rotor_rtf.ipynb (geometry-validation section)
4. Self-calibration by bundle adjustment (DREGON, Q1) — message: starting from the frame-corrected nominal, we fit positions to minimize the coherence-weighted phase residual between measured and free-field RTFs, gauge-fixed and anchored to nominal; residual drops 38.3°→28.8° with only cm-scale moves, and a synthetic control recovers ground-truth geometry to 0.36 cm — so the optimizer is right and the ~25° floor is real model mismatch (directivity/near-field), not error. — evidence: the objective (coherence-weighted, phase-wrapped, prior-anchored); before/after residual + per-mic Δ table; synthetic-control recovery. — sources: notebooks/geom_calibration.py (run_bundle_adjustment, calibrate_dregon_positions), tests/test_geom_calibration.py
5. When audio can't help: Michael's, and an honest negative result (Q2) — message: a second dataset exposes both a fixable error and a hard limit. The fixable error: its array was encoded as a *vertical* ring when the top-down photo plainly shows a *horizontal* one — corrected by inspection. The hard limit: unlike DREGON, Michael's geometry is *not identifiable from its audio*. All four rotors sit clustered below a ring mounted above them (no angular diversity to triangulate) and the sources are noisy flight harmonics, so the phase objective is degenerate — every relaxation lets the fit shrink, tilt, or fly the array away to drive predicted inter-mic delays toward zero and "match" the noise. We show this cleanly with a rigid-ring parameterization (7 DOF: translate + 3-D rotate + scale): unconstrained it flies metres away at residual ~16°; anchored, the only way it lowers residual is by tilting ~80° edge-on and halving its radius — physically impossible. Conclusion: take Michael's geometry from the photo/spec; audio refinement is future work. — evidence: the vertical→horizontal photo correction; the rigid-ring λ-sweep table (residual vs centre-move/tilt/radius) as the degeneracy proof; contrast with DREGON's well-posed case. — sources: src/data_processing/michaels.py (get_geometry, corrected to horizontal), notebooks/geom_calibration.py (extract_michaels_rotor_rtfs), the rigid-ring degeneracy analysis
6. What it recovers, and what it cannot — message: the corrected geometries (3D before/after) unblock the physics model; but low harmonic frequencies weakly constrain absolute position, so the prior carries fine detail, and a few-dB magnitude error remains that is physics (directivity/partial spatial coherence), not geometry — the honest ceiling. — evidence: 3D original-vs-refined positions (both drones); identifiability discussion; magnitude-error-floor note; a one-line methods caveat on the scipy.csd phase-sign convention. — sources: the geometry_3d artifact positions, notebooks/geom_calibration.py (CalibrationResult, procrustes), memory finding on csd phase sign
