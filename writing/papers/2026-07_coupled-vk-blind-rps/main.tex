\title{Capture Range Organizes Blind Multi-Rotor Rate Tracking: Why the
  Vold--Kalman Frequency Loop Cannot Operate Blind, and What Replaces It}

\author{Dmitrii~Mukhutdinov%
  \thanks{D.~Mukhutdinov is with the Centre for Intelligent Sensing, Queen
    Mary University of London, London, UK (e-mail: flyingleafe@gmail.com).}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Per-rotor rotational speed (RPS) parameterizes the harmonic structure of
multirotor ego-noise and conditions modern drone-audition systems, yet
synchronized telemetry is rarely recorded. We study the fully blind
recovery of all four rotor-rate trajectories of a quadrotor from its
acoustic ego-noise and organize the problem by a single quantity:
\emph{capture range}. Each stage of a staged rate estimator is
characterized by a capture range, a resolution, and an analysis bandwidth,
and the staging exists because no single mechanism has all three. Read
through that lens, the classical Vold--Kalman (VK) frequency-update loop is
structurally unable to operate blind: its capture radius is $B/(2k)$, which
at our informative harmonics is $0.13\revps$ against blind track errors of
$0.5$--$1.5\revps$, so the loop is inert --- confirmed by an instrumented
per-factor audit, by an ablation that reproduces the unrefined pipeline to
$\pm0.003\revps$, and by a label-free reconstruction referee that cannot
distinguish its output from its input. This does not contradict prior
adaptive-VK work, which either estimates instantaneous frequency outside
the filter or manages the same radius implicitly by starting with a wide
band. We replace the loop with a phase-increment maximum-likelihood tracker
whose bandwidth schedule makes the radius explicit, and we correct the
information model that motivates high-harmonic weighting: arrival-time
jitter makes per-harmonic rate errors \emph{correlated}, giving a
rank-one-plus-diagonal covariance, an irreducible variance floor
$\sigma_J^2$ that no number of harmonics can lower, and a natural
$k$-scaled bandwidth $B_k = kB_0$ under which capture range, captured
energy fraction and the weight exponent all become $k$-independent. A
progressive ladder --- locked-phase noiseless, four rotors at 0\,dB,
physically band-limited shafts, timing jitter --- separates estimator
error from benchmark artifact and establishes oracle floors
($0.055\revps$ to hold an exact initialization; ${\approx}0.2\revps$ at
0\,dB with four rotors, proved by an oracle that equals the blind result).
The same label-free referee detects and corrects real errors in the
reference telemetry of two outdoor recordings: a drifting clock
($R^2 = 0.94/0.92$) and a ${\sim}0.7\%$ rate scale, whose correction alone
improves pooled cruise error by 8.4\%. We report the reconstructed pipeline
honestly: on the full fifteen-window protocol it does \emph{not} beat the
blind baseline, winning on ramps and losing 0.3--0.5\revps{} on steady
windows.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{A}{coustic} sensing from multirotor platforms is dominated by
the platform's own noise. The broadband component of ego-noise is
approachable with conventional enhancement, but the harmonic component ---
combs of tones at integer multiples of each rotor's shaft rate, sweeping
with every control action --- is both the strongest and the most structured
part of the interference. That structure is an opportunity: if the
per-rotor speed trajectories $r_i(t)$ are known, the frequencies of every
harmonic line are known, and both classical projection methods and
rotor-conditioned neural models exploit them to
advantage~\cite{yen2023ncm,gulli2025rotorcond}.

The catch is that $r_i(t)$ is almost never available. Consumer platforms do
not expose synchronized per-rotor telemetry; research datasets either omit
it or record it at rates and alignments unusable for phase-coherent
processing. Of the corpora in common use, DREGON~\cite{strauss2018dregon}
is nearly unique in shipping motor telemetry at all, and even there the
commanded-speed track freezes before landing and must be treated with care.
The practical question is therefore: \emph{can accurate per-rotor speed
trajectories be recovered from the acoustic recording alone, reliably
enough to serve as ground-truth annotation for datasets that lack
telemetry --- and where does the recoverable accuracy saturate?}

The estimator we study builds on the Vold--Kalman (VK) order-tracking
filter~\cite{vold1993high}. Its architecture follows from one structural
fact (Section~\ref{sec:structure}): the joint likelihood over the rate
trajectories $r_i(t)$ and the per-harmonic complex amplitudes $A_{i,k}(t)$
is conditionally linear in the amplitudes --- the VK envelope solve is the
exact amplitude step --- while the rate block is violently non-convex, so
it splits into a global, discrete, magnitude-based search and a local,
continuous, phase-based refinement.

What organizes the resulting ladder, and what this paper argues should be
stated first about any such estimator, is \emph{capture range}: the largest
initialization error from which a stage still converges. Every stage buys
resolution by narrowing an analysis band, and every narrowing costs capture
range; no mechanism in the ladder has wide capture, fine resolution and
usable bandwidth at once, which is exactly why there is a ladder. Once the
ladder is read that way, a specific classical component fails a specific
arithmetic test. The VK ``refine'' step --- the loop that reads a rate
correction off the solved envelope's phase slope --- has capture radius
$B/(2k)$, which at the harmonics that carry the information is
$0.13\revps$, an order of magnitude below the errors a blind global stage
actually leaves. Under blind conditions the loop therefore does nothing at
all, which we establish three independent ways. This is a statement about
the \emph{blind} operating point, not about the prior work: the
adaptive-VK literature either estimates instantaneous frequency (IF)
outside the filter~\cite{li2020vkswt} or manages the same radius
implicitly, by starting the iteration with a deliberately wide
band~\cite{jiang2024iavkf}.

The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A capture-range account of the staged estimator}
  (Section~\ref{sec:structure}). Each stage --- blind comb scan, coarse
  full-range comb Viterbi, residual corridor DP, VK frequency update,
  Fisher-weighted phase slopes, angular resampling, phase-increment
  tracker --- is characterized by capture range, resolution and analysis
  bandwidth, and the three are shown to trade off against one another
  (Table~\ref{tab:capture}). The ladder is a consequence of that table,
  not a heuristic.
\item \textbf{A diagnosis: the VK frequency-update loop is inert under
  blind conditions} (Section~\ref{sec:vkrefine}). The arithmetic is
  $\delta_{\max} \approx B/(2k)$; the measurements are (i) an instrumented
  per-factor audit showing the fused phase-slope RMS is
  $0.003$--$0.008\revps$ against true errors of $0.55$--$1.5\revps$, with
  $0$ of $25$ harmonics in band, (ii) an ablation in which disabling the
  loop reproduces the full pipeline to $\pm0.003\revps$, and (iii) a
  label-free reconstruction referee that scores the refined and unrefined
  trajectories identically to four decimal places.
\item \textbf{A corrected information model for phase-slope weighting}
  (Section~\ref{sec:fisher}). Arrival-time jitter contributes a phase error
  proportional to $k$, which the conversion to rev/s divides out exactly:
  the per-harmonic rate errors are therefore \emph{correlated}, with
  covariance $\Sigma = \sigma_J^2 \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\!\top} +
  \operatorname{diag}(v_k)$. The relative weights survive, but the fused
  variance becomes $1/W + \sigma_J^2$ --- an irreducible floor and an
  information saturation point $k^\ast$ --- and the natural
  parameterization is a $k$-scaled bandwidth $B_k = kB_0$, under which
  capture range, captured energy fraction and the weight exponent all
  become $k$-independent together.
\item \textbf{Methodology: a label-free referee}
  (Section~\ref{sec:referee}). The VK reconstruction residual
  $\|x - \hat{x}\|/\|x\|$ needs no reference track, so it can be pointed at
  either side of the comparison. Pointed at estimators it establishes
  oracle floors and adjudicates stages that a telemetry-referenced metric
  cannot separate; pointed at the \emph{telemetry} it detects and corrects
  real defects in the reference itself --- a drifting clock and a
  ${\sim}0.7\%$ rate scale on two outdoor recordings, whose correction
  alone improves pooled cruise error by 8.4\%.
\end{enumerate}

We report negative results as results. The reconstructed pipeline built on
this analysis does not currently beat the blind baseline on the full
fifteen-window protocol; it wins on ramp windows and loses 0.3--0.5\revps{}
on steady ones, and Sections~\ref{sec:scoreboard} and~\ref{sec:discussion}
say so plainly and locate the mechanism.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Vold--Kalman order tracking}
\label{sec:related-vk}

The VK filter was introduced for high-slew-rate order extraction in
automotive testing~\cite{vold1993high} and extended shortly after to
simultaneous tracking of orders referenced to multiple independent
shafts~\cite{vold1997multi}; its filter characteristics were analyzed
in~\cite{herlufsen1999characteristics,tuma2000characteristics}. The
second-generation formulation minimizes a data-fidelity term plus a
difference-operator smoothness penalty on complex envelopes, which is the
form we build on. A line of adaptive variants followed: angular-displacement
and angular-velocity reformulations~\cite{pan2007adaptive,pan2010extended}
and kernel corrections for the imaginary-part error of incomplete complex
bases~\cite{zhu2023splitcossin}. Two members of this line are the direct
references for the present work, and they occupy opposite positions on the
question this paper is about.

\subsubsection{IF estimated outside the filter}
Li, Han and Wang~\cite{li2020vkswt} feed VK an IF obtained from a
synchrosqueezed wavelet transform (SWT) with an adaptive multi-ridge peak
detector, and then reuse that same IF curve as each component's own
order-tracking reference. The VK filter itself is adopted as an
off-the-shelf block: the paper writes the second-difference structural
equation $\nabla^2 A_k(n) = \varepsilon_k(n)$ and the data equation
$\boldsymbol{X} = \boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{B} + \boldsymbol{\delta}$, and
proceeds directly to the reconstruction $\boldsymbol{S} =
\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{B}$ --- no cost functional, no weighting factor,
no normal equations, and no statement of how the system is solved. What
matters for us is architectural rather than notational: the pipeline is
strictly feed-forward. \emph{The IF is estimated once, upstream, and
handed to VK as a fixed parameter; nothing returns from the envelopes to
the IF.} A ridge estimator has essentially unbounded capture range --- it
finds the ridge wherever it sits in the plane --- so their result, that VK
separates well when handed a good IF, is not in tension with ours. We
confirm the same thing independently: envelope quality improves
monotonically with trajectory quality (Section~\ref{sec:referee}). Our
negative result concerns a component their pipeline does not contain.

\subsubsection{IF refined inside the filter}
The iterative adaptive VK filter (IAVKF) of Jiang, Chen and
Wang~\cite{jiang2024iavkf} \emph{does} contain it, and is therefore the
closest prior art. IAVKF augments the model with a per-component initial
phase $\theta_{n0}$ and absorbs both it and the IF error into the
\emph{complex} envelope, so that the unwrapped envelope-phase derivative is
exactly the IF error; that increment is then smoothed by a second,
IF-domain VK problem and added to the carrier, inside a single loop that
also re-estimates the bandwidth. This is, mechanism for mechanism, the
``refine'' step we report inert (Section~\ref{sec:vkrefine}). The two
results are reconciled by three differences, all of which bear on capture
range. First, IAVKF peels components greedily, one at a time, and its own
stated limitation is that it ``cannot properly handle nonstationary signals
with overlapping IFs'' --- which is precisely the quadrotor twin pair, two
fundamentals under $1\revps$ apart whose $k$-th teeth are never
separable by peeling; our solve is joint, with explicit cross-track
coupling. Second, its bandwidth adaptation is a single per-component scalar
$\alpha_n$ driven to an orthogonality fixed point
$\alpha_n^{k+1} = \alpha_n^{k}\,(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_n^{k})^{\!H}
\hat{\mathbf{x}} / (\hat{\mathbf{x}}_n^{k})^{\!H}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_n^{k}$,
which increases monotonically and so contracts the band from wide to
narrow. This is exactly a capture-range schedule --- and the paper says as
much operationally, advising a smaller $\alpha_n^0$ (a wider band) ``when
the initial IF significantly differs from the true one'' --- but it is
never analyzed as one, and no capture bound is quoted. Third, and most
importantly for the argument of Section~\ref{sec:fisher}, the adapted
bandwidth carries \emph{no dependence on harmonic order}. With two or three
low-order components at 0\,dB that is harmless; with twenty-five harmonics
of four combs, where the displacement of harmonic $k$ under a rate error is
$k\delta$, a common bandwidth means the capture radius collapses as $1/k$
on exactly the harmonics the weighting favors. Our $k$-scaled bandwidth
$B_k = kB_0$ is the response, and it is a direct answer to the
bandwidth-selection problem IAVKF sets out to solve, arrived at from the
comb structure rather than from an orthogonality residual.

\subsection{Ridge extraction and tacholess speed estimation}

Because the alternative to refining an IF is estimating it outright, the
ridge literature is the relevant competitor to our global stage. Iatsenko,
McClintock and Stefanovska~\cite{iatsenko2016ridges} give the canonical
adaptive treatment: a parameter-free ridge extraction combining dynamic
path optimization with fixed-point iteration, which is the mechanism
IAVKF's and~\cite{li2020vkswt}'s front ends rely on. Automated and adaptive
ridge extraction has since been targeted specifically at rotating
machinery without a tachometer~\cite{aare2023ridge}, which is the closest
prior art to our blind setting: an adaptive edge-detection strategy plus a
core function built from the analyzed signal's own instantaneous
characteristics, so that no parameter tuning is needed. Our global stage
(Section~\ref{sec:global}) is a ridge search too, with one structural
difference that the multirotor case forces: a single-component ridge
detector has no way to prefer a shaft comb over its own blade-passage
octave, or to keep four near-coincident combs apart, so we score
\emph{comb} hypotheses --- entire integer families tied to one latent
fundamental --- rather than individual lines. Where individual lines do
overlap, ridge path regrouping~\cite{chen2017rprg} resolves the crossings
by regrouping ridge curves according to their variation rates at
intersections; our twin-pair treatment (Section~\ref{sec:pairs}) faces the
harder version of the same problem, since the twin fundamentals do not
cross transversally but stay within a filter bandwidth of each other for
whole windows, and we resolve it by a two-tone spectral fit assigned by
order rather than by continuation.

\subsection{Nonstationary harmonic analysis}

A parallel literature attacks nonstationary harmonic signals by
transform design rather than state estimation. Generalized demodulation
maps a curved instantaneous-frequency trajectory onto a constant
one~\cite{olhede2005generalized}; the fan-chirp transform applies a
common time-warp matched to a harmonically related family of
chirps~\cite{weruaga2007fanchirp}, and the velocity-synchronous linear
chirplet transform adapts the warp to a measured or estimated speed
profile~\cite{guan2019vslct}. Synchrosqueezing sharpens time-frequency
ridges for IF extraction~\cite{daubechies2011synchrosqueezed}, and
chirplet path pursuit performs a global dynamic-programming search over
chirp dictionaries for weak oscillatory
signals~\cite{candes2008chirplet}. Our pipeline contains recognizable
relatives of each: the angular-resampling refiner of
Section~\ref{sec:iterwarp} is a self-referenced fan-chirp/generalized
demodulation, and the global stage of Section~\ref{sec:global} is a
path-pursuit search over comb hypotheses. What the estimation-theoretic
framing adds is an explicit per-measurement noise model --- in particular
the per-harmonic phase diffusion of Section~\ref{sec:pikalman}, which no
fixed transform accounts for.

\subsection{Multiharmonic frequency tracking}

Recovering the reference speed without a tachometer is a recognized problem
in machine monitoring; see~\cite{peeters2019tacholess} for a review
and, e.g.,~\cite{wang2019tlot} for a mode-decomposition approach to
instantaneous angular speed estimation. These methods, like ours, extract
speed from the signal itself, but they target a single shaft and typically
operate on vibration signals with favorable SNR. Multiharmonic trackers
with explicit state-space structure go back at least to extended Kalman
formulations of comb tracking~\cite{streit1992multiharmonic}; their capture
behavior --- convergence only within a basin around the true fundamental
--- motivates both the annealing schedules and the ambiguity gating used
here. The harmonic-ambiguity structure we manage coarse-to-fine is the
acoustic analogue of integer-ambiguity resolution in carrier-phase
positioning~\cite{teunissen1995lambda}: low harmonics keep the
``revolution count,'' high harmonics add precision once the count is safe.
In the audio domain, fundamental-frequency estimation is a mature field,
and modern neural estimators are robust in
noise~\cite{han2014pitchnn,riou2023pesto}; polyphonic variants recover
multiple concurrent pitches~\cite{bittner2022basicpitch}. We evaluate
representatives of this family, and stronger drone-specific regressors, as
references on the frozen protocol (Section~\ref{sec:scoreboard}): rotor
combs are not speech- or music-like, the four fundamentals are separated by
fractions of a semitone, and per-frame salience methods do not deliver
sub-\revps{} trajectory accuracy.

\subsection{Drone audition}

Ego-noise suppression on multirotors spans blind source
separation~\cite{wang2020bss}, learned time-frequency
masking~\cite{wang2020dnntf}, and a rapidly growing single-channel deep
enhancement literature~\cite{mukhlish2023survey,liu2025edgebsrof}. Several
recent systems consume rotor-speed side information explicitly: noise
covariance estimation driven by rotor speeds~\cite{yen2023ncm} and
rotor-conditioned enhancement networks~\cite{gulli2025rotorcond} both
report consistent gains from the conditioning signal. This strand is the
consumer of our method's output: high-quality $r_i(t)$ annotation is
exactly what those systems assume and what public data mostly lacks.

\section{Signal Model and Preliminaries}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Generative model}

The data is multichannel audio $x_c(t)$ ($C \le 8$ microphones, 16\,kHz
processing rate) recorded on a quadrotor whose rotor $i$ spins at
$r_i(t)$ revolutions per second --- 74--91\revps{} in cruise for the
platforms studied here. Define the \emph{shaft phase}, the accumulated
rotation angle in radians,
\begin{equation}
  \varphi_i(t) = 2\pi \int_0^t r_i(\tau)\, d\tau ,
  \label{eq:shaftphase}
\end{equation}
so that rate is the derivative of phase. Each rotor emits a harmonic comb:
energy concentrated at integer multiples $k\,r_i(t)$\,Hz of the shaft rate,
for $k$ up to ${\sim}40$ (we cap the analysis at 6\,kHz). The generative
model every tracker in this paper implicitly assumes is
\begin{equation}
  x_c(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{R} \sum_{k=1}^{K}
  \operatorname{Re}\!\left[ A_{c,i,k}(t)\, e^{\,\mathrm{j} k \varphi_i(t)}
  \right] + n_c(t),
  \label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
with $A_{c,i,k}(t)$ slowly varying complex amplitudes --- absorbing the
harmonic magnitudes $a_k$, the initial phases $\psi_k$, and any slow phase
wander --- and $n_c$ collecting broadband aerodynamic noise and any target
sources. The unknowns split into two blocks: the rates $\{r_i\}$
(equivalently the phases $\{\varphi_i\}$) and the amplitudes
$\{A_{c,i,k}\}$. This split dictates the entire architecture
(Section~\ref{sec:structure}). Real audio adds three complications: (i)
broadband noise; (ii) \emph{per-harmonic phase noise} --- the phase of
harmonic $k$ wanders relative to $k\varphi_i$, modeled in
Section~\ref{sec:pikalman} as $\theta_k(t) = k\varphi_i(t) + b_k(t) +
\psi_k$ with $b_k$ a random walk; and (iii) four combs present at once, two
of which form a \emph{twin pair} less than 1\revps{} apart (the DREGON
cruise structure is $\{74.0, 74.7, 85.3, 86.3\}\revps$).

\subsection{Demodulation and the lock statistic}
\label{sec:demod}

Every phase-based method starts by demodulating: multiplying the audio by
a complex exponential built from the current track estimate
$\hat\varphi(t)$ and low-passing. Using $\cos\theta = \tfrac12
(e^{\mathrm{j}\theta} + e^{-\mathrm{j}\theta})$, the $k$-th harmonic
contributes to $x(t)\, e^{-\mathrm{j} k \hat\varphi(t)}$ two terms: one at
frequency ${\approx}\,k(r - \hat r)$, near DC when the track is close, and
an image at ${\approx}\,{-2kr}$, thousands of hertz away. A low-pass
filter of bandwidth $B$ (a few Hz) retains only the first and also rejects
every other harmonic $k' \ne k$, which lands near $(k'-k)\,r$, at least
${\sim}74$\,Hz away. What survives is the complex envelope
\begin{equation}
  z_k(t) = \mathrm{LP}_B\!\big[ x(t)\, e^{-\mathrm{j} k \hat\varphi(t)}
  \big] \approx \tfrac{a_k}{2}\,
  e^{\,\mathrm{j}(k[\varphi(t)-\hat\varphi(t)] + \psi_k)} + n_k(t),
  \label{eq:envelope}
\end{equation}
with $n_k$ the in-band noise, complex Gaussian of total variance
$\sigma^2$. Geometrically $z_k$ is an arrow in the complex plane: exact
track $\Rightarrow$ direction frozen at $\psi_k$; rate error $\delta$
$\Rightarrow$ rotation at $k\delta$\,Hz; per-harmonic phase noise
$\Rightarrow$ the direction random-walks even under a perfect track.

The reference-free diagnostic used throughout is the \emph{lock} statistic
\begin{equation}
  \lockk = \frac{\bigl| \tfrac1T \sum_t z_k(t) \bigr|}
                {\tfrac1T \sum_t |z_k(t)|} \in [0,1] :
  \label{eq:lock}
\end{equation}
the length of the vector sum of the arrows divided by the sum of their
lengths. Full alignment gives 1; random directions give
${\sim}1/\sqrt{N}$, a floor of 0.02--0.05 for our windows. Lock demands
alignment over the \emph{whole} window --- it is a long-horizon coherence
measure. Its measured values anchor the paper's empirical claims: real
single motors on the bench reach $\mathrm{lock}_{10} \approx
0.24$--$0.4$; free flight collapses to ${\approx}\,0.03$--$0.1$ \emph{even
when demodulating with the telemetry-truth phase}
(Section~\ref{sec:budgets}).

\subsection{Phase-noise law and Fisher weights}
\label{sec:fisher}

Two first-order facts about noisy phasors are derived once and reused
everywhere. Write $z = A e^{\mathrm{j}\theta} + n$ with $|n| \ll A$ and
rotate coordinates: $z = e^{\mathrm{j}\theta}(A + \tilde n)$, where
$\tilde n = n e^{-\mathrm{j}\theta}$ has the same statistics as $n$
(circular symmetry). Then $\arg z = \theta + \arg(1 + \tilde n / A)
\approx \theta + \operatorname{Im}(\tilde n)/A$, using $\arg(1+\epsilon)
\approx \operatorname{Im}\epsilon$ for small $\epsilon$. The noise splits
its variance evenly between real and imaginary parts,
$\operatorname{Var}[\operatorname{Im}\tilde n] = \sigma^2/2$, giving the
\emph{phase-noise law}
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{Var}[\arg z_k] \approx \frac{\sigma^2}{2A^2}
  = \frac{1}{2\,\snr_k}.
  \label{eq:phasenoise}
\end{equation}
Second, a rate error $\delta$ read from harmonic $k$ is obtained by
dividing the arrow's angular speed by $2\pi k$ (because $\arg z_k$
advances at $2\pi k\delta$\,rad/s). Dividing a measurement by $k$ divides
its standard deviation by $k$, so the Fisher information about $\delta$
carried by harmonic $k$ scales as
\begin{equation}
  w_k \propto \frac{k^2}{\operatorname{Var}[\arg z_k]}
  \propto k^2\, |z_k|^2 .
  \label{eq:fisherweight}
\end{equation}
This is the standard argument for favoring high harmonics. It rests on an
assumption --- that each harmonic's phase is corrupted only by additive
background noise, \emph{independently} of the others --- which real
recordings do not satisfy, and the next subsection corrects it.

\subsection{Correlated harmonic errors: a rank-one information floor}
\label{sec:jitter}

Model the disturbance not as phase noise but as jitter in \emph{arrival
time}: the observed shaft angle advances by $r(t)(\Delta t + n_t)$ with a
small random timing error $n_t$. Harmonic $k$ then picks up a phase error
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon_k(t) = 2\pi k\, r\, n_t ,
  \label{eq:jitterphase}
\end{equation}
which grows in proportion to $k$ --- so the premise that high harmonics
carry proportionally more information is not automatic. The consequence,
however, is not the obvious one. Writing the increment measurement of
harmonic $k$ as
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\psi_k(t) = 2\pi k\, \delta\, \Delta t
  + 2\pi k\, r\,(n_{t+1} - n_t) + \nu_k ,
  \label{eq:jitterinc}
\end{equation}
with $\nu_k$ the additive-noise contribution, and dividing by
$2\pi k \Delta t$ to obtain that harmonic's opinion about the rate error,
\begin{equation}
  \hat\delta_k = \delta
  + \underbrace{\frac{r\,(n_{t+1}-n_t)}{\Delta t}}_{J}
  + \underbrace{\frac{\nu_k}{2\pi k\,\Delta t}}_{e_k} .
  \label{eq:jitteropinion}
\end{equation}
The timing term has \emph{no $k$ left in it}: dividing by $k$ cancelled
exactly the $k$ that multiplied it in~\eqref{eq:jitterphase}. There is one
shaft and one timing error, so $J$ is the \emph{same number for every
harmonic}. The per-harmonic rate errors are correlated, not independent,
and with $\operatorname{Var}(J) = \sigma_J^2$ and
$\operatorname{Var}(e_k) = v_k$ the covariance across harmonics is
rank-one plus diagonal,
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma = \sigma_J^2\, \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\!\top}
  + \operatorname{diag}(v_k) .
  \label{eq:rankone}
\end{equation}
Solving the weighted least-squares problem under~\eqref{eq:rankone} gives
two results, and only the second is a surprise. The optimal relative
weights are unchanged, $w_k \propto 1/v_k$ --- the \emph{shape} of the
$k^2$ weighting of~\eqref{eq:fisherweight} survives. But the fused
variance does not. With $W = \sum_k 1/v_k$,
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{Var}(\hat\delta) = \frac{1}{W} + \sigma_J^2 ,
  \label{eq:varfloor}
\end{equation}
not $1/W$. There is a floor no number of harmonics can lower, because they
all share the same $J$; past the point where $1/W$ falls below
$\sigma_J^2$, extra harmonics buy nothing and the information
\emph{saturates} at a finite $k^\ast$. This matters operationally because
$W$ is exactly the quantity a Fisher-weighted stage places on the diagonal
of its normal equations (Section~\ref{sec:staged}): overstating it makes
the solve trust the data relative to the smoothness prior, so the stage
chases shaft jitter instead of smoothing it, and does so most where the
most harmonics were piled on. The correct diagonal is
$W_{\mathrm{eff}} = (1/W + \sigma_J^2)^{-1}$.

Not every phase disturbance lands in $J$. The axis that matters is not how
a disturbance scales with $k$ but whether it is \emph{shared}.
\emph{Delay-like} disturbances --- a changing direct path as the drone
translates, large-eddy turbulence, shaft jitter --- act as a fluctuating
propagation time, so their phase error is $2\pi f\,\Delta t$, proportional
to $k$, and cancels on division: these land in $\sigma_J^2$ and are
irreducible. \emph{Dispersive} disturbances --- direct/reflected
interference, scattering resonances, small-scale turbulence --- perturb
phase in a way that is not a pure delay, so the residual differs between
harmonics: these land in $v_k$ and do average down. One reassurance follows
immediately: a \emph{static} transfer function contributes a fixed phase
offset per harmonic, which a phase \emph{increment} differences away
exactly. Reverberation matters because the platform moves, not because the
room is reverberant.

\subsection{$k$-scaled bandwidth}
\label{sec:kscaled}

The same model dictates the analysis bandwidth. A fixed half-width $B$
retains harmonic $k$ only while $k|\delta| < B$, so the capture range is
$B/k$: it shrinks on exactly the harmonics the weighting favors, which is
the arithmetic behind Section~\ref{sec:vkrefine}. Setting
\begin{equation}
  B_k = k\, B_0
  \label{eq:kscaled}
\end{equation}
makes the capture range $B_0$ for every harmonic. The separation argument
survives: two rotors' $k$-th teeth are $k|r_i - r_j|$ apart, so a
$k$-scaled filter preserves exactly the discrimination the fundamental had,
while same-rotor neighbours sit $r \approx 74$--$91$\,Hz away, which
$kB_0$ does not reach until $k \approx 100$, far beyond the harmonics in
use. It also changes the exponent: wider filters admit noise in proportion
to $k$, so $\snr_k$ falls as $1/k$, $v_k \propto 1/(k P_k)$, and
\begin{equation}
  w_k \propto k\,|p_k| \quad\text{rather than}\quad k^2 |p_k| .
  \label{eq:kweight}
\end{equation}
Finally, the timing model predicts each harmonic's \emph{line} is itself
$k$ times wider, so a fixed filter captures a shrinking fraction of each
higher harmonic while a $k$-scaled one captures a constant fraction.
Capture range, captured energy fraction and the weighting exponent become
$k$-independent \emph{together} --- a sign that~\eqref{eq:kscaled} is the
natural parameterization rather than a patch. Note that this is a
different answer to the bandwidth question than IAVKF's per-component
orthogonality fixed point~\cite{jiang2024iavkf}, which adapts one scalar
per component with no order dependence; the two are compatible, since
$B_0$ could itself be adapted that way.

A final caution, which is why no fixed power of $k$ is right either.
Harmonic amplitude falls with $k$ faster than the broadband floor falls
with frequency, so $\snr_k$ drops at high $k$; at low $k$ the harmonics sit
in the rumble and wind band where the floor is large. $\snr_k$ is therefore
\emph{not monotonic} --- it peaks in the middle --- and no $k^\alpha$ law
describes it. Our own pipeline encodes this without saying so: the refine
stage starts at $k_{\min} = 6$ and the capture stage's configuration is
named \emph{midband}. Those constants are a hand-tuned
signal-to-noise-versus-$k$ curve. The principled replacement is not to
derive the weights but to \emph{measure} them: demodulate along a good
trajectory, form each harmonic's opinion $\hat\delta_k(t)$, and fit the
empirical cross-harmonic covariance as rank-one plus diagonal. The
rank-one part \emph{is} $\sigma_J^2$, whatever mixture of physics produced
it, and the diagonal \emph{is} $v_k$. One trap in that fit: the true
$\delta(t)$ is also shared across harmonics, so signal and jitter are
confounded in the rank-one term; they separate in time, since the signal is
smooth at a few hertz while the jitter is fast, so fitting the covariance
on time-high-passed opinions isolates $\sigma_J^2$. This measurement is
the S4 rung of Section~\ref{sec:ladder} and is
\pending{S4 timing-jitter measurement --- see Section~\ref{sec:s4}}.

\section{The Estimation Structure}
\label{sec:structure}

Consider the joint maximum-a-posteriori problem the whole pipeline is
solving:
\begin{equation}
  \max_{\{r_i\},\{A_{c,i,k}\}}
  \log p\big(x \,\big|\, \{r_i\}, \{A\}\big)
  + \log p\big(\{A\}\big) + \log p\big(\{r_i\}\big).
  \label{eq:map}
\end{equation}
Two structural facts dictate everything.

\emph{First, the problem is conditionally linear.} For fixed rates,
model~\eqref{eq:model} is linear in the amplitudes; with Gaussian noise
and a Gaussian smoothness prior, the amplitude block is quadratic and
exactly solvable --- that is the VK envelope solve
(Section~\ref{sec:vksolve}). In estimation-theoretic language the
amplitudes can be marginalized analytically (Rao--Blackwellization):
there is no reason ever to be approximate about the $A$-block.

\emph{Second, the rate block is multimodal at every scale.} Octave doubles
(only even harmonics audible at warmup, Section~\ref{sec:fullrange}), twin
swaps, comb-of-comb aliases, and ramps that leave any local basin. A
gradient or Kalman step converges to the nearest mode; only a discrete
global search over a quantized rate grid is trustworthy about \emph{which}
mode. The pipeline is therefore a homotopy, not an accident:
\begin{quote}
  \emph{global, discrete, magnitude-domain} (immune to phase noise,
  coarse; Section~\ref{sec:global}) $\;\to\;$ \emph{local, continuous,
  phase-domain} (precise within a basin; Section~\ref{sec:local}) $\;\to\;$
  optionally alternate with the exact $A$-solve when the reconstructed
  waveform itself is needed.
\end{quote}
The literal one-solver integration of~\eqref{eq:map} would be a
Rao--Blackwellized particle or mode tracker: discrete hypotheses over
modes, Kalman flow within each, amplitudes marginalized. The two-pass
pipeline is the practical approximation of exactly that object, and the
phase-increment tracker of Section~\ref{sec:pikalman} is its within-mode
flow. The four local-stage variants of Section~\ref{sec:local} are four
generations of the \emph{same} conditional update, distinguished only by
how honestly they model the measurement --- and the frozen-protocol
measurements of Section~\ref{sec:results} adjudicate between them.

\subsection{Capture range, resolution, bandwidth}
\label{sec:capture}

The second structural fact has an operational form that we take as this
paper's organizing quantity. Call the \emph{capture range} of a stage the
largest initialization error from which it still converges to the correct
mode. Every stage in the pipeline buys resolution by narrowing an analysis
band, either literally (a demodulating low-pass) or effectively (a longer
analysis window, a finer grid); and every narrowing costs capture range,
because a rate error $\delta$ displaces harmonic $k$ by $k\delta$\,Hz and
a band of half-width $B$ retains it only while $k|\delta| < B$. The
recurring bound is therefore
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{\max} \;\approx\; \frac{B}{k},
  \label{eq:captureband}
\end{equation}
halved when the criterion is that the line stay within the band's
\emph{half}-width. Resolution moves the opposite way: a magnitude peak of
width ${\sim}1/T_w$ localizes frequency only to
${\sim}1/(T_w\sqrt{\snr})$, so precision demands long windows or narrow
bands, i.e.\ small capture range. And a third quantity is independent of
both: the \emph{trajectory bandwidth} a stage can follow --- a stage
solving one estimate per 16\,s window cannot represent a takeoff ramp, no
matter how wide its capture range or how fine its resolution.

\section{Global Stage: Comb-Matched Dynamic Programming}
\label{sec:global}

\subsection{Whitened comb score and Viterbi path}
\label{sec:viterbi}

The first stage ignores phase entirely. Compute a whitened log-magnitude
spectrogram $M(f,t)$ --- whitening divides out the smooth spectral
envelope (running median over frequency) so combs stand out. For a
candidate shaft rate $c$, score the comb hypothesis at frame $t$ by
reading the spectrogram at the comb's tooth positions,
\begin{equation}
  S(c, t) = \sum_{k \le K} w_k\, M(k c,\, t),
  \label{eq:combscore}
\end{equation}
and find the best path $c(t)$ by dynamic programming over a discretized
rate grid:
\begin{equation}
  V(c, t) = S(c, t) + \max_{c'} \big[ V(c', t{-}1) - \gamma\, |c - c'|
  \big],
  \label{eq:viterbi}
\end{equation}
with $\gamma$ a smoothness penalty. By Bellman's principle this recursion
finds the \emph{exact global maximizer} of $\sum_t S(c(t),t) - \gamma
\sum_t |\Delta c(t)|$ over all grid paths --- no initialization, no
basins. That is its superpower: it cannot be trapped by a bad starting
point, and magnitude is robust to phase decoherence because it discards
phase. Its limits are equally structural: precision is capped by the
frequency-bin width and the smoothing prior --- a magnitude peak of width
${\sim}1/T_w$ localizes frequency only to ${\sim}1/(T_w\sqrt{\snr})$ per
window, far worse than coherent phase tracking over the same record ---
and a pair-mean formulation cannot split twin rotors. Measured on the
frozen protocol, the DP ridge attains 0.95--1.15\revps{} per steady
window: the workhorse figure that every refinement stage tries, and on
steady DREGON windows mostly fails, to improve
(Section~\ref{sec:displaced}).

\subsection{Blind seeding: band cap, residual re-scan, stage guard}
\label{sec:seeding}

Blind operation requires seeds for the per-rotor grids. We scan a
whitened time-averaged spectrum with a comb matched filter over a dense
fundamental grid, capping the dictionary at 1.2\,kHz: above that band,
comb aliases of \emph{lower} fundamentals begin to outscore true combs,
and the cap alone repaired the corpus where naive scanning failed
outright. Because a strong rotor can shadow a weaker one at a nearby
fundamental, seeding proceeds in rounds: after each acceptance the seeded
comb is subtracted in the whitened domain and the residual re-scanned.
Finally, the multi-stage refinement ladder that runs downstream is
wrapped in a per-track guard: a stage whose output drops a track's comb
\emph{occupancy} --- the fraction of its harmonics found above the local
noise floor --- is reverted for that track. The guard addresses a failure
mode in which a re-captured track lands on a \emph{louder, wrong} comb;
raw residual confidence increases during exactly this failure, so
occupancy rather than confidence is the reliable signal.

Each component is load-bearing and isolatable. Removing the scan-band cap
returns the cross-drone recording to a 22\revps{} failure (the scan seeds
an alias); removing the residual re-scan leaves its fourth rotor
permanently unseeded (3.24\revps{} pooled --- the guard cannot invent a
missing seed); disabling the stage guard lets late refinement re-capture
one rotor onto a louder neighboring comb (9.7\revps{} on that rotor)
while every internal confidence measure improves.

\subsection{Full-range ramp extension}
\label{sec:fullrange}

The seeder's hypothesis class is \emph{constant} bases estimated from the
time-averaged (cruise-dominated) spectrum, and the tracking DP stages of
the ladder only search $\pm 6\revps$ around them. Two failure modes of
the blind pipeline on full-coverage evaluation live exactly here, and
neither is a filtering problem.

\subsubsection{Octave ambiguity at warmup}
On idle/warmup windows of the cross-drone recording (true shaft
31--41\revps) the spectrum contains \emph{only even} shaft harmonics ---
the blade-passage lines of two-bladed propellers (62.5/72.3/82\,Hz and
multiples), with measured zero odd-line energy. A comb score is then
bimodal in octave: pure comb evidence \emph{cannot} decide whether the
observed line family is the shaft comb or the blade-passage comb of a
shaft one octave down, and the scan's octave-up promotion commits to the
doubled bases (pooled error railing at 33--36\revps). The discriminator is
physical, not spectral: for a candidate base $b$, if the line \emph{at}
$b$ is stronger than the line at $2b$, then $b$ is itself the blade-pass
comb and the shaft is $b/2$ --- at cruise the blade-pass line $2b$
dominates the shaft line $b$. The measured line-strength ratios
$v(b)/v(2b)$ (8192-point whitened time-mean, maxima within
$\pm1.5$\,Hz) are ${\le}\,0.93$ on every cruise window and
${\ge}\,1.87$ on every warmup window; a threshold of 1.4 on the median
over unique seed bases halves all bases (and drops the seed's
gate-calibration, which was fitted on the rejected bases).

\subsubsection{Coarse full-range Viterbi}
To make ramps reachable, a coarse slope-tolerant Viterbi pass is
prepended, producing a time-varying coarse trajectory $c(t)$ over the full
physical range 12--120\revps{} (grid step 0.5\revps; the floor 12
excludes the low-$c$ GCD-alias zone where all $k\le8$ teeth fall into
low-frequency rumble). Critically it runs on a \emph{short-window}
whitened spectrogram at the native frame rate (2048-point FFT at 16\,kHz:
7.8\,Hz bins, 0.128\,s window, 32\,ms hop): window-averaged surfaces
smear a 30\revps-per-second takeoff ramp into invisibility, whereas at
this resolution the $k\le8$ comb sweep is ${\sim}1$ bin per frame per
tooth (an 8192-point FFT smears it over ${\sim}26$ bins). The score is
the \emph{rigid additive union template} $r_0(c) = c + (\text{bases} -
\operatorname{median}(\text{bases}))$ --- all four rotors moved together
--- evaluated with a positive-half-tooth contrast: the mean whitened
value over on-teeth positions $k(c+\text{offset})$ minus the mean
\emph{positive} whitened value over half-teeth $(k-\tfrac12)(c +
\text{offset})$, which penalizes sub-multiple aliases without the
whitening-dip artifact of a signed contrast. Per-frame scores are
soft-normalized (floor 0.3$\times$ the global median contrast) so
weak-evidence ramp frames still express their preference, lightly smoothed
over 3 frames, and chained by~\eqref{eq:viterbi} with $\gamma =
0.4$\revps{} per frame. For windows halved by the octave test the grid is
restricted to $\operatorname{median}(\text{bases}) \pm 16\revps{}$ ---
in the even-harmonics-only regime full-range magnitude evidence is
structurally octave-attracted --- with an adaptive tooth count $k \le
360/c$ (clipped to $[8,24]$) so every candidate is scored on a comparable
band.

\subsubsection{Trust gates}
The coarse DP must not override a good constant seed. Two gates decide,
per window: a \emph{steady} gate --- path span $p_{98} - p_{2} <
8\revps{}$ means there is no ramp to track (steady windows measure
${\le}\,6.5$; the smallest true ramp measures 10.0), so the exact
constant blind init is used, removing coarse wobble on every steady
window; and a \emph{distrust} gate --- $|\operatorname{median}(c(t)) -
\operatorname{median}(\text{bases})| > 5\revps{}$ means the DP abandoned
the seed structure, so fall back to the constant init. The distrust gate
was forced by a measured failure: on two cross-drone maneuver windows the
seeds are asymmetric (a duplicated pair at 74\revps{} plus singles at
82.7/92.35), the rigid template let the DP park the tight pair on the
dominant 91.5\revps{} comb, shifting the path median by $+17\revps{}$ and
turning a 1.18\revps{} window into 15.6; on every well-behaved window the
shift is ${\le}\,1.1$, on the broken ones 16--17.

\subsubsection{Energy-timed takeoff bridge}
Through the middle of a takeoff ramp the narrowband evidence vanishes
under the broadband spool-up: the DP times the low-to-high transition
${\sim}1.5$\,s late, or idles on an alias when an interfering source
buries the idle comb. Acoustic power, however, tracks rotor rate steeply.
When the DP path contains a two-plateau jump exceeding 20\revps{}
\emph{and} the window has a ${\ge}\,1$\,s low-energy idle phase (the
takeoff-from-idle signature; keyed on energy alone the bridge mangled a
maneuver window), the pre-cruise path is rebuilt from the 50--200\,Hz
rotor-rumble band energy profile --- monotone in rate even under the
speech and white-noise interferer recordings, whose 2--6\,kHz band is
flooded. Idle frames receive $c_{\mathrm{lo}}$ from a constant-rate
re-scan of the idle frames restricted to $c \le 0.45\,c_{\mathrm{hi}}$
(excluding the $c_{\mathrm{hi}}/2$ sub-multiple attractor; the DP's own
low plateau is junk exactly when a masker hides the idle comb); transition
frames follow the power law $c_{\mathrm{lo}}
(c_{\mathrm{hi}}/c_{\mathrm{lo}})^{\alpha}$ in the normalized energy
$\alpha$; a catch-up hold at $c_{\mathrm{hi}}$ (the median DP path over
sustained-high-energy frames) persists until the DP path rejoins within
5\revps.

\subsubsection{Ladder init and measured effect}
The ladder initialization anchors on the \emph{seed bases}, not on the
path: $r_0^{(i)}(t) = \text{base}_i + \big(c(t) -
\operatorname{median} c\big)$, clamped at zero --- so any residual
constant DP offset cancels, and gated windows reduce \emph{exactly} to
the constant blind init (steady windows are bit-identical to the
unmodified blind pipeline). The standard refinement ladder runs on top,
unchanged. Table~\ref{tab:fullrange} lists the measured per-window

every ramp/warmup catastrophe (15--36\revps) lands at 2.9--4.0\revps,
and pooled full-coverage DREGON error drops from 6.80 to 1.81\revps{}
(Section~\ref{sec:scoreboard}).

\section{Local Stage: One Conditional Update, Four Generations}
\label{sec:local}

\subsection{The coupled VK envelope solve: the exact amplitude step}
\label{sec:vksolve}

Fix the phase tracks $\hat\varphi_i(t)$. Model~\eqref{eq:model} is then
linear in the amplitudes, and the VK filter is nothing more than
penalized linear least squares,
\begin{multline}
  \min_{\{A\}} \sum_t \Big( x(t) - \sum_{i,k}
  \operatorname{Re}\big[A_{i,k}(t)\, e^{\mathrm{j}k\hat\varphi_i(t)}\big]
  \Big)^{\!2} \\
  + \sum_{i,k} \rho_{i,k}^2 \sum_t \big| \Delta^2 A_{i,k}(t) \big|^2 ,
  \label{eq:vk2}
\end{multline}
with $\Delta^2$ the second-difference operator. In the frequency domain a
second-difference penalty acts as a fourth-order low-pass on each
$A_{i,k}$ whose equivalent bandwidth shrinks as $\rho_{i,k}$ grows, so
$\rho_{i,k}$ \emph{is} the per-harmonic analysis bandwidth in disguise,
chosen from a target $-3$\,dB bandwidth by the standard
relation~\cite{tuma2000characteristics}.

\subsubsection{Normal equations and their structure}
Index the tracks by $m = (i,k)$, $m = 1,\ldots,M$, and collect each
track's carrier into a diagonal matrix and its envelope into a vector,
\begin{align}
  C_m &= \operatorname{diag}\big(e^{\mathrm{j}\theta_m[0]},\ldots,
  e^{\mathrm{j}\theta_m[N-1]}\big), \nonumber\\
  x_m &= \big(x_m[0],\ldots,x_m[N-1]\big)^{\!\top},
  \label{eq:carriermat}
\end{align}
with $\theta_m[n] = k\hat\varphi_i(t_n)$. Stacking
$x = (x_1;\ldots;x_M)$, $C = [\,C_1\;\cdots\;C_M\,]$ and
$R = \operatorname{blkdiag}(\rho_1^2 D_2^{\mathsf H}D_2, \ldots,
\rho_M^2 D_2^{\mathsf H}D_2)$, objective~\eqref{eq:vk2} is
$J(x) = \|y - Cx\|^2 + x^{\mathsf H}Rx$, and setting
$\partial J/\partial x^{\mathsf H} = 0$ gives the normal equations
\begin{equation}
  \big(C^{\mathsf H}C + R\big)\, x = C^{\mathsf H} y .
  \label{eq:normaleq}
\end{equation}
The right-hand side is the recording demodulated by each track's own
carrier, i.e.\ exactly~\eqref{eq:envelope} before low-passing. The matrix
on the left has only two kinds of entry. The block linking track $m$ to
track $m'$ is
\begin{equation}
  C_m^{\mathsf H} C_{m'} = \operatorname{diag}
  \big(e^{\,\mathrm{j}(\theta_{m'}[n] - \theta_m[n])}\big)_{n=0}^{N-1},
  \label{eq:crossblock}
\end{equation}
which is \emph{diagonal}: two tracks meet only at the same sample. Its
entries have unit magnitude and an angle that advances at the frequency
gap between the tracks. The blocks $\rho_m^2 D_2^{\mathsf H}D_2$ are banded
in time with stencil $[1,-4,6,-4,1]$, and always within one track.

\subsubsection{Why time-major ordering}
Number the unknowns time-major, $\operatorname{idx}(m,n) = nM + m$ --- every
track at sample 0, then every track at sample 1. Cross-track entries then
sit at index distance $< M$ and within-track entries at distance $M$ and
$2M$, so nothing reaches further than
\begin{equation}
  q = 2M
  \label{eq:halfband}
\end{equation}
and the system is Hermitian positive definite \emph{banded} with
half-bandwidth $2M$, factorized by banded Cholesky in $O(N q^2)$. Order
the unknowns the other way --- all of track 1, then all of track 2 --- and
the same cross-track entries land at distance $N$, a quarter of a million
for a 16\,s window: equally sparse, and useless to a banded solver. The
interleaving is not a storage trick, it is what makes the cost linear
rather than cubic in record length.

\subsubsection{Coupling, and when it becomes singular}
Retaining the off-diagonal blocks~\eqref{eq:crossblock} is what allows two
tracks separated by less than a filter bandwidth to divide energy
correctly instead of both claiming it --- the ``explaining-away'' that
makes VK the classical tool for crossing orders, and that a quadrotor's
interlaced combs make mandatory. It is also where the failure mode lives.
When two tracks nearly coincide, the angle
in~\eqref{eq:crossblock} barely turns over the window, the entry stays near
$1$, and the two columns of $C$ become nearly parallel: the system is
near-singular and its minimum-norm solution is a large,
mutually-cancelling pair of envelopes. The reconstruction then contains
more energy than the recording. We observe this directly --- on a DREGON
ramp window the reconstruction residual ratio is $1.82$, meaning the
harmonic model \emph{added} 82\% energy rather than explaining any
(Section~\ref{sec:referee}) --- and it is why a rate-refinement stage
that improves the trajectory can improve the amplitude model by a factor
of two without touching the amplitude solver at all.

Solving~\eqref{eq:vk2} at audio rate for $R \times K \approx 100$ tracks
is unnecessary: the envelopes are narrowband by construction. We
demodulate each track as in~\eqref{eq:envelope} (zero-phase), decimate to
an envelope rate $\fsenv$ of 100--200\,Hz, and solve on the decimated
grid. The decimated problem is not an approximation of convenience:
restricted to envelopes bandlimited below $\fsenv/2$ --- the subspace the
smoothness prior already enforces --- minimizing~\eqref{eq:vk2} at audio
rate and minimizing its decimated counterpart are the same problem, since
every component the demodulating low-pass discards is orthogonal to that
subspace and shifts the objective by a constant. The coupling coefficient
survives decimation exactly for those pairs whose instantaneous
separation falls below $\fsenv/2$ --- precisely the pairs that interfere.
Tracks are partitioned into coupling groups by union--find on this
predicate; each group yields a Hermitian positive-definite \emph{banded}
system under time-major ordering, factorized by banded Cholesky, and
far pairs are pruned with no measurable trajectory effect. The full-rate
signal still anchors each outer round: convergence is monitored by the
audio-rate residual ratio of the full reconstruction, and carriers are
re-derived at audio rate from every updated trajectory. Together these
choices reduce a nominal $10^6$-unknown sparse problem to a sequence of
banded solves (runtime figures in Section~\ref{sec:runtime}).

Note precisely what this solves and what it does not: it is the optimal
\emph{amplitude} step \emph{conditioned on the phase tracks being right}.
The remainder of this section is the history of doing the other
conditional step --- the rate update given the envelopes --- correctly.

\subsection{Generation 1: the VK frequency update is inert under blind
  conditions}
\label{sec:vkrefine}

The classical refine step reads rate corrections from the solved
envelopes: if the track is off by $\delta$, each $\hat A_{i,k}(t)$
rotates at $k\delta$\,Hz (the arrow logic of Section~\ref{sec:demod}), so
read the rotation, fuse over $k$ with the weights
of~\eqref{eq:fisherweight}, nudge $\hat r$, and iterate; each outer
iteration multiplies the correction by a Wiener-shrink confidence
$g = \snr/(1{+}\snr)$, clips it (default $0.5\revps$), passes it through a
periodogram gate, and re-derives the carriers. This is the same loop as
IAVKF's~\cite{jiang2024iavkf} --- envelope solve, unwrapped
envelope-phase derivative, smoothed IF increment, rebuild carriers --- with
the difference that our corrections are fused across a comb tied to one
latent fundamental rather than estimated per free component.

\subsubsection{The arithmetic}
The envelope solve only reconstructs content within its bandwidth
$B \approx 1.5$\,Hz of the assumed tooth position. A rate error $\delta$
displaces harmonic $k$ by $k\delta$\,Hz; once $k\delta \gtrsim B/2$ the
true energy lies \emph{outside} the solved envelope, the arrow contains
noise, and no amount of iteration recovers. The basin is the
half-width case of~\eqref{eq:captureband},
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{\max} \approx \frac{B}{2k},
  \label{eq:basin}
\end{equation}
which at $B = 1.5$\,Hz and the informative harmonics $k \ge 6$ is under
$0.13\revps$. The blind global stage delivers errors of
$0.55$--$1.5\revps$. The loop is therefore being asked to correct errors
four to twelve times its own capture radius --- and, being an in-band
estimator, it does not fail loudly. It reports convergence.

\subsubsection{Confirmation 1: an instrumented per-factor audit}
Every multiplicative factor in the step was logged across three 16\,s
windows $\times$ five refine rounds $\times$ four rotors. The suspected
suppressors are all innocent: the no-comb gate never fired, the
$0.5\revps$ clip never engaged, the Wiener shrink is a mild ${\times}2$,
and the smoothing solve attenuates by only ${\times}1.2$--$1.6$. The raw
Fisher-fused slope RMS entering the smoother is $0.003$--$0.008\revps$
against true track errors of $0.55$--$1.5\revps$ --- a shortfall of two to
three orders of magnitude that exists \emph{before} any gate or
smoother touches it. The cause is exactly~\eqref{eq:basin}: at the final
bandwidth of 1.5\,Hz, \emph{$0$ of $25$ harmonics} have $|k\delta|$ in
band anywhere in any window. Meanwhile $61$--$93\%$ of the same harmonics
lie inside the much wider $\pm45$\,Hz demodulation brickwall --- and that
wider band is what the gate tests. So the gate sees a healthy comb, the
step sees nothing, and the loop reports that it has converged in its own
narrow-band terms. It has: the residual is invisible to it.

\subsubsection{Confirmation 2: ablation}
Disabling the frequency update entirely (\texttt{no\_refine}) reproduces
the full pipeline's per-window error to within $\pm0.003\revps$ on every
window tested. Five refine rounds move the trajectory by
$\Delta \approx 0.002$--$0.004\revps$ per round, and on one real cruise
window the five rounds together make the answer very slightly
\emph{worse}. The stage is not weakly helpful; it is a no-op.

\subsubsection{Confirmation 3: the label-free referee}
The third check is independent of any reference track. A VK envelope set
is a \emph{readout} of (audio, trajectory, configuration), so the
reconstruction residual $\|x - \hat{x}\|/\|x\|$ measures how much of the
recording the harmonic model explains, with no telemetry involved
(Section~\ref{sec:referee}). Running the refine rounds changes that
residual by $\approx 0$ --- on one real cruise window it is identical to
four decimal places (0.5774 before, 0.5774 after). A trajectory update
that genuinely improved the track would improve the envelopes, because the
envelopes are downstream of it; a phase-increment update on the same
window improves the residual to 0.5300. Three instruments, three different
kinds of evidence, one verdict.

\subsubsection{Why this is not a contradiction of prior work}
The result is specific to the \emph{blind} operating point, and it is
consistent with both VK references of Section~\ref{sec:related-vk}.
\cite{li2020vkswt} contains no such loop at all: its IF comes from an SWT
ridge detector whose capture range is the whole time--frequency plane.
IAVKF~\cite{jiang2024iavkf} contains the loop but begins with a
deliberately wide band --- its own guidance is to reduce $\alpha_n^0$ when
the initial IF is far from the truth --- so its bandwidth adaptation
\emph{is} a capture schedule, and the fact that it is never described as
one is precisely what makes the failure easy to inherit. Its components
are also few and of low order, where~\eqref{eq:basin} is not yet binding.
Annealing the harmonic range in our own loop (small $K_{\max}$ and a stiff
trajectory prior early, widening as the track locks --- the
capture-versus-resolution compromise of extended Kalman comb
trackers~\cite{streit1992multiharmonic}) widens the basin in the same way,
and is the correct instinct; the point of this paper is that the
schedule should be derived from~\eqref{eq:basin} and the twin separation
rather than tuned, which is what Section~\ref{sec:pikalman} does.

The conclusion is narrow and practical. ``VK refine'' $=$ (exact linear
amplitude solve) $+$ (a local, narrow-basin rate step). Keep the first
half: the envelope solve is still the reconstruction engine and the
referee (Section~\ref{sec:referee}) is built on it. Drop the second half;
it costs runtime and buys $\pm0.003\revps$.

\subsubsection{A ruled-out explanation: phase noise}
One hypothesis had to be eliminated before~\eqref{eq:basin} could be
credited, because it predicts the same symptom. If shaft jitter broadened
each harmonic line beyond the envelope band, the energy would be
out-of-band for a reason having nothing to do with track error. It does
not. On a synthetic in which comb and noise are kept separate and VK is
run with the \emph{exact} trajectory, the fraction of comb energy captured
at $B = 1.5$\,Hz is $100.0\%$ at $0.2\times$, $1\times$ and $3\times$ the
nominal shaft jitter (up to a per-rotor std of $6.9\revps$), and
$97.6\%$ at 0\,dB broadband SNR. The reason is structural: VK demodulates
\emph{along the trajectory}, by $k\!\int\! r\,dt$, so a correct
demodulation removes the frequency modulation by construction and the line
is narrow in the envelope domain however much the shaft wanders. Phase
noise would bite only if we demodulated at a constant frequency.

This test has a limitation that we state rather than hide, because it is
what motivates the S4 rung of Section~\ref{sec:ladder}: the synthetic
locks every harmonic's phase to $2\pi\!\int\! r\,d\tau$, so its only phase
variation is a deterministic function of the trajectory, which a
trajectory-following demodulator removes by construction. Independent
arrival-time jitter --- the $J$ term of~\eqref{eq:jitteropinion} --- is
\emph{not} a function of $r(t)$, so no trajectory-following demodulator
removes it, and our synthetic contains none of it. The conclusion above is
therefore established for frequency-modulation smearing and unsupported
for timing jitter.

\subsection{Generation 2: Fisher-weighted phase slopes (stage D)}
\label{sec:staged}

Stage D skips the envelope solve: demodulate directly per harmonic with a
fixed $B = 3$\,Hz band, compute per-sample IF errors from the unwrapped
phase derivative,
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\delta}_k(t) = \frac{1}{2\pi k}\,\frac{d}{dt}
  \operatorname{unwrap}\big(\arg z_k(t)\big),
  \label{eq:staged}
\end{equation}
and fuse across $k$ and channels with the Fisher
weights~\eqref{eq:fisherweight} under a smoothness prior.

\subsubsection{From the cost to a pentadiagonal system}
Every track gives one noisy opinion per sample about the same unknown
curve $\delta(t)$, so the fusion is
\begin{equation}
  J(\delta) = \sum_t \sum_k w_k[t]\,
  \big(\hat\delta_k[t] - \delta[t]\big)^2
  + \lambda\, \|D_2\,\delta\|^2 .
  \label{eq:stagedcost}
\end{equation}
Expanding the inner sum at one $t$ and completing the square, with
\begin{equation}
  W[t] = \sum_k w_k[t], \qquad
  \bar\delta[t] = \frac{\sum_k w_k[t]\,\hat\delta_k[t]}{W[t]},
  \label{eq:suffstat}
\end{equation}
gives $\sum_k w_k(\hat\delta_k - \delta)^2 = W[t](\delta[t] -
\bar\delta[t])^2 + \mathrm{const}$: the weighted mean and the total weight
are \emph{sufficient statistics}, so a hundred harmonics collapse to two
numbers per sample with no approximation. Differentiating the collapsed
cost yields
\begin{equation}
  \big(\operatorname{diag}(W) + \lambda\, D_2^{\mathsf T} D_2\big)\,
  \delta = W \odot \bar\delta ,
  \label{eq:pentasys}
\end{equation}
the same shape as~\eqref{eq:normaleq} --- a data term on the diagonal plus
a smoothness term --- but with one unknown per sample instead of $M$, so
the matrix is real, symmetric and \emph{pentadiagonal} ($q = 2$), interior
row $[\lambda,\, -4\lambda,\, W[t]{+}6\lambda,\, -4\lambda,\, \lambda]$,
positive definite as soon as any $W[t] > 0$, and solved by the same banded
Cholesky. The behaviour where evidence is missing is the reason to solve a
system rather than smooth $\bar\delta$ directly: as $W[t] \to 0$ the row
is governed by $\lambda D_2^{\mathsf T}D_2$, whose null space is the
straight lines, so the solution interpolates across the gap instead of
collapsing to zero.

\subsubsection{What breaks}
The machinery is sound; the inputs are not. Stage D has no per-measurement
variance model --- the weights assume the phase-noise
law~\eqref{eq:phasenoise} \emph{always} holds, i.e.\ that $z_k$ is
signal-dominated. Three things break it, and the third is the subject of
Section~\ref{sec:jitter}. (i) Any real broadband floor: the stage stalls
already at $+10$\,dB SNR, because \texttt{unwrap} commits cycle slips on
noise-dominated samples, and slips are catastrophic unbounded errors that
quadratic weighting does not reject. (ii) Per-harmonic phase diffusion,
whose phase slope is white noise scaled by the diffusion rate ---
informative about nothing, yet weighted by $k^2|z_k|^2$ as if it were pure
track error. (iii) The rank-one term: $W[t]$
in~\eqref{eq:pentasys} is exactly the quantity that~\eqref{eq:varfloor}
says is overstated, so the solve trusts the data relative to the prior and
chases shaft jitter, worst where the most harmonics were pooled. The
corrected diagonal is $W_{\mathrm{eff}}$, and harmonics should be capped
where the information saturates rather than at an arbitrary $k_{\max}$;
the collision thresholds, which are in absolute hertz today, must then
scale with $k$ too, or the wider high-$k$ filters would quietly begin
admitting neighbours. Stage D is the cautionary tale: right weights, no
model of when the weights lie.

\subsection{Generation 3: angular resampling (iter\_warp)}
\label{sec:iterwarp}

Generalized demodulation~\cite{olhede2005generalized} and angular
resampling attack the capture problem. Change the time variable to
\emph{revolutions of the current track}: define $\theta$ by sampling the
audio at equal increments of $\hat\varphi$ (constant samples per
revolution). Substituting $\varphi = \hat\varphi + 2\pi\varepsilon$, with
$\varepsilon$ the track error in revolutions, harmonic $k$ becomes
\begin{equation}
  \cos\big(k\varphi(t) + \psi_k\big) =
  \cos\big(2\pi k\,\theta + 2\pi k\,\varepsilon(\theta) + \psi_k\big):
  \label{eq:warp}
\end{equation}
in the warped variable it is a tone at exactly \emph{order} $k$ (cycles
per revolution), phase-modulated only by the slowly varying error
$\varepsilon$. A long FFT over the warped window --- the order spectrum
--- turns a nearly constant rate error $\dot\varepsilon$ into a peak
\emph{offset}: the tone appears at order $k(1 + \dot\varepsilon)$, so the
fractional rate error is the offset divided by $k$. This is a
\emph{global search within a band}, immune to the basin
problem~\eqref{eq:basin}, because peak-picking scans the whole search
band rather than following a gradient. Sub-bin accuracy comes from
parabolic interpolation: fitting $p(u) = \alpha u^2 + \beta u + \gamma$
through the log-power values $(a, b, c)$ at bins $-1, 0, +1$ gives
$\alpha = (a + c - 2b)/2$, $\beta = (c - a)/2$, and vertex $u^\ast =
-\beta/(2\alpha)$, i.e.,
\begin{equation}
  u^\ast = \frac{a - c}{2(a - 2b + c)} .
  \label{eq:parabola}
\end{equation}

The refinement proceeds over a ladder of rungs --- the harmonic-ambiguity
schedule made concrete. Round 1 uses low orders (1--6) with long windows
(3\,s) and a wide search band ($\pm2.5\revps$) to keep the revolution
count; subsequent rounds are orders 4--12 / 1.5\,s / $\pm1.8$, 8--24 /
0.75\,s / $\pm1.2$, and 12--40 / 0.375\,s / $\pm0.6$, under the invariant
that the incoming error times the highest order stays below half a cycle
per revolution --- so a peak is never confused with its neighbor.
Measured, iter\_warp is the only pre-Kalman method that captures offsets
under noise ($+1\revps \to 0.04$ at 0\,dB) and the first to achieve
genuine partial lock on real bench motors. Its residual weakness is
within-window stationarity: wandering rates (and wandering twin
separations) smear the long-window peaks --- exactly the regime a proper
state-space treatment handles.

\subsection{Generation 4: the phase-increment tracker (pi\_kalman)}
\label{sec:pikalman}

The phase-increment tracker keeps the demodulation of
Section~\ref{sec:demod} but changes both the measurement and the model.
The measurement is the \emph{phase increment} between consecutive
envelope frames,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\psi_k(t) = \arg\big( z_k(t)\, \overline{z_k(t{-}1)} \big),
  \label{eq:increment}
\end{equation}
the wrapped angle between successive arrows --- computable without ever
unwrapping a long record, so there is no cycle-slip catastrophe: one
increment can at worst be wrong by itself. The model adds what free
flight demonstrably has: the phase of harmonic $k$ is
\begin{equation}
  \theta_k(t) = k\varphi(t) + b_k(t) + \psi_k,
  \label{eq:diffmodel}
\end{equation}
with $b_k$ a Brownian motion of rate $q_k$ (rad$^2$/s; coherence time
$\tau_k \sim 2/q_k$) --- the decoherence channel measured on real rotor
audio (Section~\ref{sec:budgets}). Increments stay informative about the
rate error $\delta r$ long after absolute phase has decohered, because
the diffusion enters the likelihood as \emph{measurement noise} instead
of breaking the signal model:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}[\Delta\psi_k(t)] = 2\pi\,\Delta t\, k\, \delta r(t),
  \label{eq:incmean}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{Var}[\Delta\psi_k(t)] =
  \underbrace{\frac{1 - \operatorname{sinc}(2B\Delta t)}%
  {\snr_k}}_{\text{noise term}}
  + \underbrace{c_{\mathrm{diff}}\, q_k\, \Delta t}_{\text{diffusion
  term}} .
  \label{eq:incvar}
\end{equation}
Both variance factors deserve their derivations; both were load-bearing
bugs before they were fixed.

\subsubsection{Noise term}
The increment is a difference of two phase readings, each of variance
$v = 1/(2\,\snr)$ by~\eqref{eq:phasenoise}. But the two readings share
\emph{filtered} noise: after a low-pass of bandwidth $B$, the noise
autocorrelation at lag $\Delta t$ is $\rho = \operatorname{sinc}(2B\Delta
t)$ (the Fourier transform of a flat band $[-B, B]$ is a sinc). The
variance of a difference of correlated variables is
$\operatorname{Var}[\phi_1 - \phi_2] = 2v(1 - \rho)$, giving the noise
term of~\eqref{eq:incvar}. With $B = 6$\,Hz and $\Delta t = 16$\,ms,
$1 - \operatorname{sinc}(0.192) \approx 0.06$: the increment sees only
6\% of the per-sample noise variance. Omitting this factor overpredicts
the noise ${\sim}16\times$, leads to the conclusion that all residual
variance is already explained, and estimates $q_k = 0$ everywhere ---
precisely the failure of the first implementation.

\subsubsection{Diffusion term}
The Brownian phase passes through the same low-pass before being
differenced. Compute the variance through spectra: a Wiener process has
power spectral density $q/(2\pi f)^2$; differencing over $\Delta t$
multiplies the spectrum by $|1 - e^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi f \Delta t}|^2 = 2(1 -
\cos 2\pi f \Delta t)$; the ideal band keeps $|f| \le B$. Substituting
$u = 2\pi f \Delta t$,
\begin{align}
  \operatorname{Var}
  &= \int_{-B}^{B} \frac{q}{(2\pi f)^2}\, 2\big(1 - \cos 2\pi f \Delta
  t\big)\, df \nonumber\\
  &= q\,\Delta t \cdot \frac{2}{\pi}
  \int_0^{2\pi B \Delta t} \frac{1 - \cos u}{u^2}\, du
  \;=\; q\,\Delta t\; c_{\mathrm{diff}} .
  \label{eq:cdiff}
\end{align}
As $B \to \infty$, $\int_0^\infty (1 - \cos u)/u^2\, du = \pi/2$, so
$c_{\mathrm{diff}} \to 1$ --- the unfiltered Brownian increment variance
$q\Delta t$, as it must. At our $B\Delta t$, $c_{\mathrm{diff}} \approx
0.19$.

\subsubsection{From the cost to a tridiagonal system}
Sections~\ref{sec:vksolve} and~\ref{sec:staged} both ended at a banded
linear system, and so does this one. Two things constrain the unknown
$\delta[t]$: every phase increment is a noisy measurement of it, and a
rotor's rate error should not jump between frames. Writing $h_k = 2\pi
k\,\Delta t$ for the gain that turns a rate error into a phase step,
$R_{ck}[t]$ for the measurement variance~\eqref{eq:incvar}, and
$q = \sigma_p^2 \Delta t$ for the random-walk strength,
\begin{multline}
  J(\delta) = \sum_t \sum_{c,k}
  \frac{\big(\Delta\psi_{ck}[t] - h_k\,\delta[t]\big)^2}{2 R_{ck}[t]} \\
  + \sum_t \frac{\big(\delta[t] - \delta[t{-}1]\big)^2}{2q}
  + \frac{\delta[0]^2}{2 P_0} .
  \label{eq:pikcost}
\end{multline}
Because every measurement touches exactly one time index, the first term
collapses --- as in~\eqref{eq:suffstat} --- into two numbers per frame,
\begin{equation}
  I[t] = \sum_{c,k} \frac{h_k^2}{R_{ck}[t]}, \qquad
  m[t] = \sum_{c,k} \frac{h_k\,\Delta\psi_{ck}[t]}{R_{ck}[t]},
  \label{eq:infoform}
\end{equation}
the total precision available at frame $t$ and the precision-weighted
evidence. Differentiating, with $D_1$ the first-difference operator,
\begin{equation}
  \Big(\operatorname{diag}(I) + \tfrac{1}{q}\,
  D_1^{\mathsf T} D_1\Big)\,\delta = m ,
  \label{eq:tridiagsys}
\end{equation}
whose stencil $[-1, 2, -1]$ makes it symmetric \emph{tridiagonal}, half
bandwidth $1$. The in-band noise floor $\snr_k$ in $R_{ck}$ is estimated
from an \emph{off-comb} demodulation at $k\hat\varphi + 2\pi\Delta_{\!f}t$
($\Delta_{\!f} = 3$\,Hz, off every comb line), and frames whose envelope
sits at or below that floor are gated out.

\subsubsection{The Kalman filter \emph{is} the banded solve}
We do not factor~\eqref{eq:tridiagsys}; we run a forward pass and a
backward pass. These are not a different algorithm. \emph{The forward
Kalman recursion is forward elimination on~\eqref{eq:tridiagsys}, and the
Rauch--Tung--Striebel smoother is the back substitution.} For a
tridiagonal matrix that elimination is the Thomas algorithm, which is
banded Cholesky at half-bandwidth $1$. Concretely, the forward step is one
line of Gaussian algebra --- posterior precision is the sum of precisions,
\begin{equation}
  P_{t|t}^{-1} = P_{t|t-1}^{-1} + I[t]
  = P_{t|t-1}^{-1} + \sum_{c,k}
  \frac{(2\pi \Delta t\, k)^2}{\operatorname{Var}[\Delta\psi_{c,k}(t)]},
  \label{eq:kalman}
\end{equation}
and the backward pass distributes the whole window's information to every
frame. Two practical reasons prefer the recursive form anyway: it streams,
so it needs no full matrix and could run causally; and it produces the
posterior variances $P_{s}[t]$ for free, which a plain solve would not.

Table~\ref{tab:threestages} states the consequence. The three refinement
stages of this paper are one algebra --- a data term on the diagonal plus
a difference-operator prior, solved by banded elimination --- and they
differ in only two respects that matter. The \emph{order of the prior}:
$D_2$ prefers straight lines and $D_1$ prefers flat ones, so the
phase-increment tracker is the more conservative extrapolator where
evidence is missing. And the \emph{bandwidth schedule} that decides which
measurements exist at all, which Section~\ref{sec:vkrefine} showed to be
decisive.

\subsubsection{Closing the loop}
After the smoother, $\hat r \mathrel{+}= \delta r$ (clipped) and the next
outer iteration re-demodulates. Three ingredients close the loop.
\emph{(i) Data-estimated $q_k$:} run a first pass with $q = 0$, measure
the robust (median-absolute-deviation) excess of the residual variance of
$\Delta\psi_k$ over the SNR-predicted noise term, and set $q_k =
\max(0, \text{excess})/(c_{\mathrm{diff}}\,\Delta t)$ --- a
method-of-moments estimate of each harmonic's diffusion. On bench motors
this yields the first quantitative decoherence budget of the campaign:
$q \approx 0$ for $k \le 5$ (fully coherent), $\tau_k$ of order 1\,s for
$k = 10$--$40$ (Section~\ref{sec:budgets}).
\emph{(ii) Effective sample size:} the frame rate (62.5\,Hz) oversamples
the band ($2B = 12$\,Hz), so successive measurements are not independent;
the information sum in~\eqref{eq:kalman} is deflated by the factor
$2B\Delta t$ --- the band contains only $2B$ independent complex samples
per second.
\emph{(iii) Ambiguity gating:} an increment is unambiguous only while
$k\,|\delta r|\,\Delta t < \tfrac12$ cycle, so harmonics enter
coarse-to-fine exactly as iter\_warp's rungs --- but the schedule now
\emph{emerges} from a stated invariant plus the measured $\tau_k$, rather
than hand-tuned windows; a wrap guard additionally drops near-$\pi$
increments. Multi-rotor operation refines rotors sequentially inside each
iteration under a per-frame twin rule: measurements of harmonic $k$ of
rotor $i$ are gated out at frames where any harmonic of any other rotor
comes within $B + B_{\mathrm{guard}}$ of it.

Stated approximations: the noise of adjacent increments is treated as
independent (it is MA(1) --- neighbors share one envelope sample); the
wrapped-phase likelihood is treated as Gaussian (fine above the SNR
gate); $q_k$ is time-constant within an iteration. All are absorbed in
practice by the process/measurement variance split.

\subsection{Twin pairs: the winding argument and the pair-joint mode}
\label{sec:pairs}

When rotors $i, j$ sit within the band at harmonic $k$, the envelope is a
two-phasor sum $z = a_1 e^{\mathrm{j}\theta_1} + a_2
e^{\mathrm{j}\theta_2}$. Write
\begin{equation}
  \arg z = \theta_1 + \operatorname{Im}\,
  \log\!\big(1 + \tfrac{a_2}{a_1}\, e^{\mathrm{j}(\theta_2 -
  \theta_1)}\big).
  \label{eq:winding}
\end{equation}
If $a_2 < a_1$, the phasor $1 + \rho e^{\mathrm{j}\beta}$ traces a circle
that does \emph{not} enclose the origin as the beat phase $\beta$
advances, so the log term is periodic with zero net winding: over a beat
cycle, the average rotation rate of $z$ equals $\dot\theta_1$ --- the
increments follow the stronger tone \emph{entirely}, biased by up to half
the split, and no reweighting fixes that. The per-frame twin gate
discards most such measurements, but on tight pairs (split below a
threshold) the collided band contains a usable two-phasor mixture, not
garbage. In \emph{pair-joint} mode, each pair's self-collided harmonics
are demodulated once by the pair-mean phase $k\bar\varphi$ in a band wide
enough for $\pm k \cdot \mathrm{split}/2$; per short window the two-tone
spectrum is fitted (two-peak pick with parabolic
interpolation~\eqref{eq:parabola}, channel-incoherent power) and the two
line frequencies are assigned to rotors \emph{by order} (higher line
$\to$ faster rotor --- immune to search-band ambiguity), yielding direct
per-rotor rate observations that feed the same per-rotor smoothers as
unit-harmonic measurements. The mixture's phase increments are
deliberately \emph{not} used as a pair-mean measurement --- that is the
content of~\eqref{eq:winding}.

\section{A Label-Free Referee}
\label{sec:referee}

Every number in Section~\ref{sec:results} is scored against telemetry, and
telemetry is not truth. It has a clock, a quantizer, and a calibration,
and Section~\ref{sec:telemetry} shows that on two of our four recordings
all three were wrong. Before that can be established --- or before two
estimator variants that differ by less than the reference's own noise can
be ranked --- an instrument is needed that does not consult the reference
at all.

\subsection{The reconstruction residual}
\label{sec:residual}

The instrument follows from the two-block structure. A VK envelope set is
a \emph{readout} of the triple (audio, trajectory, configuration): given a
trajectory, the envelopes are the exact conditional minimizer
of~\eqref{eq:normaleq}, and nothing about them is fitted to any reference.
Define
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{R}\big(r(\cdot)\big) =
  \frac{\|x - \hat{x}_{r}\|}{\|x\|},
  \qquad
  \hat{x}_r = \textstyle\sum_{i,k} \operatorname{Re}
  \big[\hat A_{i,k}\, e^{\mathrm{j}k\varphi_i}\big],
  \label{eq:referee}
\end{equation}
the fraction of the recording that a harmonic model built on trajectory
$r$ fails to explain (fixed configuration, $k = 1..30$). Lower is better;
$\mathcal{R} > 1$ means the reconstruction \emph{added} energy, the
near-singular cancelling mode of Section~\ref{sec:vksolve}. Two properties
make it usable as a referee. It is label-free, so it can be pointed at
either side of an audio-versus-telemetry comparison. And it is monotone in
trajectory quality --- which is not an assumption but the thing that must
be checked, and is (Table~\ref{tab:referee}): on every window a
phase-increment refinement improves it, and on the DREGON ramp window it
converts a diverging reconstruction ($1.8191$) into a working one
($0.9898$).

First, VK-refine changes the amplitude model by nothing --- identical to

trajectory is fitted to the audio while the telemetry is independent ---
which is exactly why the telemetry investigation below is conducted with
controls rather than with this number alone.

\subsection{Oracle floors}
\label{sec:oracle}

The same instrument gives the ladder its floors. An \emph{oracle} arm
reconstructs a rotor's siblings from ground-truth trajectories, subtracts
them from the audio, and refines the remaining rotor on the residual; the
gap between the oracle and the blind chain is then the part of the error
attributable to inter-rotor interference, and the oracle's own score is a
bound that no sibling-handling scheme can beat. This decomposition is what
turns ``the estimator is not accurate enough'' into a statement with a
denominator, and it is what Section~\ref{sec:s2} uses to prove --- rather
than assert --- that $0.2\revps$ is the floor of a 0\,dB four-rotor
benchmark.

\subsection{Auditing the reference: telemetry calibration}
\label{sec:telemetry}

Pointed at the telemetry, the referee scans a candidate correction (a time
shift, a dilation, a rate scale) and reports which value of it lets the
audio be explained best. Only the telemetry moves; the audio and the
estimator are fixed. Applied to the two outdoor recordings of the
cross-drone platform (FLY124, FLY125; DJI Matrice~100, 8-mic ring,
flight-controller telemetry at ${\approx}29$\,Hz), it finds two distinct
defects.

\subsubsection{A drifting clock, not a constant lag}
Scanning a pure time shift per cruise window returns lags that are not
constant but \emph{drift linearly with recording time}: on FLY124 four
cruise windows give $-61.83$ to $-31.77$\,ms with OLS slope
$+0.654$\,ms/s and intercept $-86.1$\,ms ($R^2 = 0.94$); on FLY125 nine
windows give $-158.35$ to $-105.54$\,ms with slope $+0.377$\,ms/s and
intercept $-172.1$\,ms ($R^2 = 0.92$). The discriminating control is the
model comparison: the linear model's residual RMS is $2.90$ and
$4.49$\,ms against $12.04$ and $16.19$\,ms for the best constant lag ---
a $4.1\times$ and $3.6\times$ improvement, on 4 and 9 points respectively.
This is a clock-rate error, so the loader's telemetry-to-audio map
$W = d\tau + (\text{ts}_0 - \text{offset})$ absorbs it as
$d_{\mathrm{new}} = d_{\mathrm{old}}/(1-b)$,
$\text{offset}_{\mathrm{new}} = \text{offset}_{\mathrm{old}} - a/(1-b)$
for a fit $\mathrm{lag}(t) = a + bt$, giving corrected constants
$(-20.753813,\, 1.001654644)$ and $(-26.337849,\, 1.005178509)$.

\subsubsection{A rate scale}
Independently, the telemetry reads \emph{low} at cruise on both
recordings. Fitting one global gain over all 13 cruise windows, excluding
the twin-pair rotors the audio cannot resolve, gives
$0.698\% \pm 0.069$ and $0.706\% \pm 0.034$ --- two independent fits
agreeing to $0.008$ percentage points. Three controls constrain the model.
(i) \emph{Per-rotor} offsets are not identifiable: the between-rotor
spread ($0.099$, $0.051\revps$) is smaller than the within-rotor scatter
($0.155$, $0.109$), and adding per-rotor constants buys $0$--$4\%$ of RMS
over one global constant. (ii) The competing hypothesis --- a per-rotor
lag between the ESC-reported and physical speed --- is refuted three ways:
splitting by the sign of $\dot r$ gives implied $\tau$ of
$-3.6$ to $+0.8$\,ms, a per-rotor lag scan returns values consistent with
zero, and on a joint $(\tau_r, b_r)$ grid the offset axis carries $100\%$
of the explanatory power ($\Delta\mathcal{R} = 0.0104$ / $0.0092$) while
the lag axis carries $0.0000$ to four decimals. The arithmetic also kills
it a priori: cruise $\dot r$ has RMS $15$--$19\revps^2$ but mean
$-0.08$ to $+0.43\revps^2$, so a $30$\,ms lag biases the mean by
$0.0003$--$0.013\revps$, two to three orders below the disputed
$+0.5$--$0.9$. (iii) After applying the fitted constants, a re-run on all
13 windows leaves a residual lag RMS of $3.40$ / $3.22$\,ms --- the level
of the fit's own residual, with no monotone per-window trend --- and a
residual rate offset of $-0.054$ / $-0.009\revps$, i.e.\ about a tenth of
what was removed.

\subsubsection{What the correction is, and is not}
The form is multiplicative for two reasons, one physical and one
algebraic. Physically, the published frames span ground idle and warm-up:
an additive $+0.6\revps$ would invent motion on a stationary rotor,
whereas a scale vanishes as $r \to 0$. Algebraically, the global gain is
degenerate with a sample-clock error, which is exactly multiplicative:
with $\varepsilon_a$ an audio sample-rate error, $\varepsilon_t$ a
telemetry-clock error and $\varepsilon_r$ an ESC scale error,
\begin{equation}
  d - 1 \approx \varepsilon_a - \varepsilon_t, \qquad
  g \approx -\varepsilon_a - \varepsilon_r ,
  \label{eq:clockdegeneracy}
\end{equation}
and the two recordings are self-consistent under either
$\varepsilon_r = 0$ or $\varepsilon_a = 0$. We therefore state the
conclusion in its weak form: this is a \emph{label-for-this-audio}
correction, not proof that the flight controller is miscalibrated. It is
also worth noting what~\eqref{eq:clockdegeneracy} independently implies:
$\varepsilon_a$ is common to all four rotors, so no clock hypothesis can
produce a per-rotor difference --- corroborating (i) above from a
different direction. Honest limits: a direct additive-versus-multiplicative
discriminator has no statistical power here (the per-rotor optima are
dominated by per-rotor acoustic strength, with observed spread
$6$--$17\times$ what either model predicts), and the FLY124 constants rest
on four cruise windows.

\subsubsection{DREGON: a different defect}
The indoor corpus shows neither problem --- its telemetry is logged at
1\,kHz and needs no realignment --- but it has one of its own, visible
from the CSV alone. Its \texttt{motors\_measured} channel lies on a
\emph{reciprocal-period lattice}: a period counter uniform in $1/v$ at
$42\,\mu$s, giving a quantization step of $0.28\revps$ at $80\revps$, and
updating at only $44$--$45$\,Hz with zero-order hold between updates. A
32\,ms increment is therefore one or two lattice steps, which is the
documented $\pm0.6\revps$ fast jitter of the DREGON reference, and it is
why every raw-referenced score in Section~\ref{sec:results} carries a
jitter floor that a smoothed-reference score does not.

\subsubsection{Effect on the scoreboard}
Correcting the two constants moves the reference and, because the same
constants set where each window's audio is cut, the audio. The two cannot
be separated from each other by this experiment; both together improve
pooled FLY124 cruise error from $2.644$ to $\mathbf{2.421}\revps$
($-8.4\%$), with every seed-stable window improving individually. It is
the largest single improvement reported anywhere in this paper for the
cross-drone recording, and no estimator was changed to obtain it. The
caveat is equally instructive and is documented in
Section~\ref{sec:scoreboard}: the 86\,ms audio realignment also flips one
window's blind seed, which costs more than the correction gains.

\section{Experiments: A Progressive Ladder}
\label{sec:validation}

\subsection{Structure of the ladder}
\label{sec:ladder}

The experiments are organized by difficulty, one nuisance per rung, so
that the rung on which a method breaks identifies the assumption it
violates and --- equally important --- so that error can be attributed
either to the estimator or to the benchmark. Rungs S1--S3 are synthetic
and are stated as \emph{floors}: what is achievable, not what we achieve.
S4 adds the nuisance that motivates Section~\ref{sec:jitter} and is not
yet measured. The real-data rungs then bridge to flight.

\subsubsection{S1 --- locked phase, noiseless: the estimator floor}
\label{sec:s1}
One rotor, an additive $1/k$ comb on a synthetic rate trajectory at mean
$80\revps$, every harmonic's phase locked to $2\pi\!\int\! r\,d\tau$, and
no broadband noise. This rung asks only whether the estimator can hold and
recover a trajectory it is given the information to hold. Initializations
are truth plus constant offsets $\{0, 0.3, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0\}\revps$; the
$0$ cell measures the floor and the others measure capture range.

\subsubsection{S2 --- four rotors at 0\,dB: inter-rotor interference}
\label{sec:s2}
Four rotors at the DREGON twin structure
$\{74.0, 74.7, 85.3, 86.3\}\revps$ with a broadband floor, gridded over
comb-versus-noise SNR $\{0, 10, 20, 40\}$\,dB. The distinguishing arm is
the \emph{oracle} of Section~\ref{sec:oracle}: siblings reconstructed from
ground-truth trajectories and subtracted before refinement. Comparing
oracle against blind at each SNR separates ``the sibling combs are in the
way'' from ``the noise is in the way'', and the SNR at which the two arms
meet is the point past which no sibling-handling scheme can help.

\subsubsection{S3 --- physically band-limited shafts: the benchmark
  artifact}
\label{sec:s3}
Identical to S2 except that the rate trajectory is low-passed at a cutoff
$f_c \in \{5, 8, 12, \infty\}$\,Hz \emph{before} the audio is synthesized
and before the ground truth is defined. A rotor has inertia and cannot
follow a white drive; a benchmark whose generator ignores that, and which
then point-samples the reference onto the analysis frame grid without an
anti-alias filter, charges the estimator for trajectory content no
estimator could recover. The sweep over $f_c$ quantifies how much of a
reported error is this artifact. The calibration control is
\emph{real} telemetry: DREGON's 1\,kHz measured-speed track has $82\%$ of
its AC power below 5\,Hz against the unlimited synthetic's $97\%$, i.e.\
real shafts are, if anything, \emph{rougher} than the synthetic --- so the
roughness is realistic and it is the \emph{sampling} convention that is
wrong. Results are reported against both conventions.

\subsubsection{S4 --- arrival-time jitter}
\label{sec:s4}
The rung that tests Section~\ref{sec:jitter}, and the one this draft does
not yet have. S1--S3 all lock every harmonic's phase to the shaft integral,
so their only phase variation is a deterministic function of the
trajectory --- which a trajectory-following demodulator removes by
construction (Section~\ref{sec:vkrefine}). Independent arrival-time jitter
is not a function of $r(t)$, so no such demodulator removes it, and none of
our synthetics contain any. S4 injects a timing error $n_t$ of controlled
variance into the shaft-angle advance, then measures the empirical
cross-harmonic covariance of the per-harmonic opinions
$\hat\delta_k(t)$~\eqref{eq:jitteropinion} on time-high-passed data and
fits it as rank-one plus diagonal. Three predictions are on the record and
will be scored: that the rank-one component $\sigma_J^2$ is non-zero on
\emph{real} data and not merely on the injected synthetic; that the fused
variance saturates at a finite $k^\ast$ rather than continuing to fall as
$1/W$; and that under the $k$-scaled bandwidth~\eqref{eq:kscaled} the
empirically optimal weight exponent moves from $k^2$ toward $k$, as
predicted by~\eqref{eq:kweight}. Measured values:
\pending{S4 --- $\sigma_J^2$ on synthetic and on DREGON/FLY124 cruise;
$k^\ast$; fitted weight exponent under fixed vs.\ $k$-scaled bandwidth}.

\subsubsection{Real data}
\label{sec:realdata}
Two corpora, deliberately unlike each other. \textbf{DREGON} is indoor and
reverberant, a quadrotor in free flight in a room, 8 microphones at
44.1\,kHz resampled to 16\,kHz, with per-rotor telemetry logged at 1\,kHz
--- accurate in time, but quantized onto the reciprocal-period lattice of
Section~\ref{sec:telemetry}. Three free-flight recordings are used
(no interferer, speech interferer, white-noise interferer).
\textbf{Michael's recordings} (FLY124, FLY125) are outdoor, an unrelated
DJI Matrice~100 with an 8-mic ring and flight-controller telemetry at
${\approx}29$\,Hz --- coarse in time, and, as Section~\ref{sec:telemetry}
established, initially miscalibrated in both clock and scale. Between them
they separate reverberation from wind, indoor from outdoor, and a fast
accurate reference from a slow one. Four intermediate real rungs isolate
the mechanism of the coherence collapse: single motors on the bench
(8\,ch, setpoints $50$--$90\revps$, no ground-truth phase, so scored by
recovered-IF stability and lock); all four motors running statically on
the ground; quasi-stationary hover; and free flight. The successive deltas
decompose the collapse into multi-rotor masking, aerodynamic loading, and
translation.

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

With ground truth (S1--S3): edge-trimmed IF MAE (rev/s); shaft-phase
circular RMSE after fitting the initial phase that maximizes coherence,
plus accumulated phase drift in revolutions; and per-harmonic coherence
\begin{equation}
  C_k = \Big| \tfrac1T \textstyle\sum_t e^{\,\mathrm{j} k
  (\hat\varphi(t) - \varphi(t))} \Big|, \qquad
  k \in \{1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 40\}.
  \label{eq:ck}
\end{equation}
Without ground truth (every rung, so that the synthetic-to-real crossing
is made on one unchanged metric): the lock statistic~\eqref{eq:lock} of the
demodulated envelopes. Two decoherence diagnostics ride on the same
envelopes: (a) an off-comb noise floor --- re-demodulating at
$k\hat\varphi + 2\pi\Delta_{\!f} t$ ($\Delta_{\!f} = 3$\,Hz, off every
comb line) yields $\mathrm{lock}_k^{\mathrm{off}}$, and
$\mathrm{lock}_k^{\mathrm{corr}} = \sqrt{\max(\mathrm{lock}_k^2 -
(\mathrm{lock}_k^{\mathrm{off}})^2,\, 0)}$ is the bias-corrected lock;
(b) a coherence-time curve $L(T)$ --- the mean over disjoint spans of
length $T$ of $|\mathrm{mean}_{\mathrm{span}}\, z| /
\mathrm{mean}|z|$, $T \in \{0.125, \ldots, 8\}$\,s --- which decays with
$T$ at the rate the comb decoheres and separates fast phase noise (short
plateaus) from slow drift (long plateaus).

\subsection{The frozen full-coverage protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

End-to-end trackers are compared on a frozen protocol designed to remove
every degree of evaluator freedom: a content-addressed dataset pin
(\texttt{beatvk-valid-raw}) covering three DREGON free-flight
recordings (no interferer, speech interferer, white-noise interferer;
8\,mics, 44.1\,kHz resampled to 16\,kHz) and one recording of an
unrelated DJI Matrice~100 (FLY124; 8-mic ring, flight-controller
telemetry at ${\approx}29$\,Hz); a fixed manifest of fifteen 16\,s
windows tiling the recordings \emph{in full} --- each DREGON recording
contributes its takeoff-ramp window plus two cruise windows, FLY124
contributes six windows including warmup spool-up and a maneuver window;
and a fixed scorer: per-window permutation-invariant (PIT) mean absolute
error of the four estimated trajectories against \emph{raw} linearly
interpolated telemetry, pooled across windows. Smoothing arms exist in
the scorer but apply to \emph{predictions only}, never to the reference;
all headline numbers use the unsmoothed arm. Estimator outputs are
stitched over the manifest windows; the scorer edge-clamps outside
covered spans. The ``steady'' subset (DREGON w1/w2 of each recording;
FLY124 w3--w5) excludes each recording's ramp window and FLY124's warmup.
The raw reference matters: DREGON's measured-speed track carries
$\pm0.6\revps$ zero-mean fast jitter --- now explained, as the
reciprocal-period lattice of Section~\ref{sec:telemetry} --- so
raw-referenced scores include a jitter floor that smoothed-reference
scores do not. Earlier guarded-blind results (0.688 pooled DREGON cruise,
1.027 FLY124 cruise on 20\,s windows) were scored against smoothed
telemetry and are consistent with the ${\sim}1.0$ steady-window raw scores
reported below; the 1.027 figure additionally predates the telemetry
recalibration and comes from a different (20\,s sweep) protocol, so it is
quoted only for continuity
(\pending{20\,s guarded-blind bar re-run on the corrected loader}).

One consequence of Section~\ref{sec:telemetry} is a protocol change and
must be stated up front, because it partitions the numbers below. The
frozen pin was rebuilt on the corrected telemetry constants
(\texttt{@268c766} $\to$ \texttt{@54849c13}). Window boundaries, window
count and regime tags are unchanged on all fifteen windows, and the nine
DREGON windows are bit-identical in audio \emph{and} labels, so every
DREGON number is directly comparable across the two builds. The six FLY124
windows are not: their labels rose $+0.23$ to $+0.56\revps$ and their
audio moved $86.188$\,ms earlier in the recording (a pure displacement,
cross-correlation $r = 0.9997$, identical on all six). FLY124 figures
below are therefore labelled \emph{pre} or \emph{post}; the neural
reference rows of Table~\ref{tab:scoreboard} were scored on the \emph{pre}
build and their FLY124 column is
\pending{neural scoreboard re-scored on \texttt{@54849c13}}.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{S1--S3: floors, and how much of the error is the benchmark}
\label{sec:floors}

\subsubsection{S1 --- the estimator floor is 0.055, not 0.4}
Given an exact initialization and asked only to hold it, the
phase-increment tracker ends the window $0.055\revps$ from truth. Constant
offsets up to $\pm1\revps$ are removed to ${\approx}0.1$; the capture
boundary lies near $1.5\revps$, and $2.0$ is outside it. (This is the
four-rotor configuration, where the twin gate withholds the low harmonics
that carry the widest capture; a single isolated comb at 0\,dB captures
$+2.0$ to $0.08$, Table~\ref{tab:ladder}.) Precision is
therefore not intrinsically limited by the estimator at this rung, which
is what makes the remaining rungs interpretable: whatever error appears
later is attributable to something the rung added.

Two properties of the estimator surface here and both are design
constraints. First, \emph{iteration is harmful}. Every schedule peaks
after \emph{one} application and then degrades monotonically ---
$0.167 \to 0.196 \to 0.226 \to 0.244 \to 0.268\revps$ over five repeats
--- because each pass injects ${\approx}0.05\revps$ of fresh estimation
noise that accumulates additively in the track. The correct design is one
pass, no convergence loop, no polish. Second, the residual is
\emph{SNR-independent}: from a shape-deficient initialization it lands at
$0.413$ at 0\,dB, $0.334$ at 10\,dB, $0.337$ at 20\,dB and $0.336$ at
40\,dB. Whatever it is, it is not measurement noise.

\subsubsection{S3 --- it is a bandwidth limit, and partly a benchmark
  artifact}
Decomposing that residual by frequency band settles it. Below 0.5\,Hz the
estimator recovers $99.9\%$ of the ground truth's trajectory power
(error-to-truth power ratio $0.001$--$0.006$); at 2--4\,Hz the ratio is
${\approx}0.2$; above 8\,Hz it is ${\approx}1.0$, i.e.\ nothing is
recovered. The ground truth holds $70.7\%$ of its power below 0.5\,Hz, but
$47\%$ of the \emph{residual} sits in the 8--16\,Hz band --- the frame-grid
Nyquist edge. Neither cause is the estimator's: the generator's
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck drive is white to the 250\,Hz synthesis rate, which a
real rotor's inertia cannot follow, and the reference is point-sampled onto
the 31.25\,Hz frame grid with no anti-alias filter, folding it all back in.
Band-limiting the shaft before synthesis moves the numbers as predicted:
$f_c = \infty$ gives $0.354$, $f_c = 12$\,Hz gives $0.277$, $f_c = 8$\,Hz
gives $0.221$, $f_c = 5$\,Hz gives $0.186$ (all at 40\,dB, one pass). The
calibration control keeps this honest --- real DREGON telemetry has
\emph{more} high-frequency content than the synthetic ($82\%$ of AC power
below 5\,Hz against $97\%$) --- so the synthetic's roughness is fair and it
is the sampling convention that inflates the score.

\subsubsection{S2 --- what remains is inter-rotor interference, until it
  is not}
On clean band-limited four-rotor data an oracle that reconstructs and
removes the siblings from ground truth reaches $0.081\revps$, against
$0.193$ for the plain chain and $0.157$ for a blind single-rotor residual
refinement: at high SNR, sibling removal is worth roughly a factor of two
and the blind chain leaves most of it unclaimed. At 0\,dB the two arms
\emph{meet} --- oracle $0.201$ against blind $0.203$ --- so additive noise
dominates and no sibling scheme can do better. This is the useful form of
the result: $0.2\revps$ is the floor of a 0\,dB four-rotor benchmark, and
it is proved by an oracle rather than asserted.

\subsection{Ladder: capture and lock}
\label{sec:ladderresults}

Table~\ref{tab:ladder} compresses the ladder. Reading down the rows, each
generation of the local stage widens the capture basin and survives one
more real-world nuisance. VK-refine stalls everywhere beyond its
$B/(2k)$ basin~\eqref{eq:basin}. Stage D is exact when clean and
collapses under any broadband floor (cycle slips). iter\_warp captures
under noise but degrades under per-harmonic jitter it cannot model, and
on real free flight its long-window peaks smear. pi\_kalman is the only
method that captures a $+2\revps$ offset at 0\,dB (0.08), and on real
bench motors it is the only stage whose answer is

\subsection{The negative result: the reconstructed chain does not beat the
  blind baseline}
\label{sec:negative}

The analysis of Sections~\ref{sec:vkrefine}--\ref{sec:pikalman} was
assembled into a reconstructed chain: drop the inert frequency update,
decouple the per-rotor trajectories by residual corridor DP, and finish
with one sibling-subtracted phase-increment pass. Tuned on the synthetic
batteries and on two real windows it improved pooled error by
$12$--$23\%$ at a fraction of the runtime. On the full fifteen-window
protocol it does not hold, and Table~\ref{tab:negative} is the result.

The per-window breakdown names the mechanism rather than leaving it as a
pooled regression. On DREGON the chain wins $2$ of $9$ windows and loses
$7$; the two wins are ramp windows (e.g.\ $3.272 \to 3.197$) and every
loss is a steady window, degraded by $0.3$--$0.5\revps$. Stage-by-stage,
the corridor DP is a \emph{no-op} on 8 of the 9 DREGON windows --- its
surface-quality gate skips all four rotors, so the hypothesis that it
displaces an already-correct rotor is refuted --- and the entire
regression is the final sibling-subtracted pass. Its per-rotor
decomposition is diagnostic: on each steady window one or two rotors
acquire a $1.2$--$2.4\revps$ \emph{negative} bias while the others barely

capture range again, from the other side: DREGON cruise is two tight twin
pairs, so the siblings' reconstruction explains only about a third of the
RMS, and a wide first band ($12$\,Hz at $k \le 4$, i.e.\ $\pm12\revps$ at
$k = 1$) lets a single-track solve on a residual that still contains the
twin's comb slide onto it. On ramp windows the failure is different: the
reconstruction diverges, its guard trips, and the ungated fallback runs a
wide tracker on the plain audio --- not a decoupling step at all.

Two lessons, both worth more than the pooled number. First, a stage tuned
on synthetic batteries plus two real windows generalized to neither
corpus; the fifteen-window protocol exists precisely to catch that, and it
did. Second, a truth-free acceptance gate --- decline the pass when the
sibling reconstruction diverged, when the proposed move exceeds
$0.5\revps$ (an order of magnitude above the $0.3$--$0.5\revps$ sibling
bias the pass exists to remove), or when it lands on a sibling's comb ---
restores parity: pooled $1.822$ / $3.912$, a maximum single-window loss of
$+0.019$ against $+2.702$ ungated. That gate rejects $54$ of $60$
per-rotor proposals, which is the honest description of what it achieves:
it makes the pass a near-no-op, i.e.\ ``never worse than baseline'', not a
new source of gain. We note, and decline to use, a rule that scores
slightly better still: comb confidence before and after. It is better on
these windows and it points the wrong way in principle --- the destination
of a bad capture event is a \emph{stronger} comb, so confidence rises
exactly when it should fall.

For continuity with the earlier evaluation regime: under the 20\,s
guarded-blind protocol scored against smoothed telemetry, the blind
tracker attains 0.680/0.701/0.744\revps{} pooled on the three DREGON
free-flight recordings (twin pair resolved on two of three) and
1.027\revps{} on FLY124 cruise (per-rotor 0.67/1.19/1.22/1.03, all four
captured); the same-clip telemetry-initialized references are 0.729 and
0.282\revps. The FLY124 numbers there predate the telemetry recalibration.

\subsection{The label correction, and a seeding lottery}
\label{sec:labelresult}

Section~\ref{sec:telemetry}'s correction is scored here because it is the
one intervention in this paper that improves the cross-drone recording
without touching the estimator. Cleanly separated on FLY124 cruise pooled,
baseline chain: pre-recalibration $2.644$; the label and alignment
correction alone $\mathbf{2.421}$ ($-0.223$, $-8.4\%$); the incidental
blind-seeding flip it triggers $+1.571$; as measured $3.992$. Every
seed-stable window improves individually ($5.070 \to 4.995$,
$0.983 \to 0.747$, $3.375 \to 2.954$), consistent with labels now
${\approx}0.56\revps$ closer to truth and $31$--$62$\,ms better timed. The
refinement chain shows the same split ($2.702 \to 2.519$, $-6.8\%$).

The flip deserves its own sentence because it is a lesson about blind
seeding rather than about labels. The $86$\,ms audio realignment changes
exactly \emph{one} window's blind seed, and it does so because the
residual re-scan there was adjudicating a $0.06\%$ tie between two
candidates; the realignment moved both scores by ${\approx}1.5\%$ and
reversed the order. The seed that was ``good'' before was not a property
of the seeder --- it won a coin flip. Restoring it puts that window at its
best value ever ($0.986$), and because the lost rotor is also the one a
cross-window corroboration rule depends on, its loss propagates to a
second window. A rotor-band plausibility prior (reject a residual
candidate that would stretch the seed set's max/min beyond $1.45$)
removes the implausible candidate and nothing else on all fifteen windows,
but honestly it only prevents a catastrophic answer; the remaining choice
is still decided by $4.7\%$ of a low-contrast score. Low-contrast residual
ranking is the structural weakness of blind seeding, and none of the work
in this paper fixes it.

\subsection{Decoherence budgets}
\label{sec:budgets}

The data-estimated diffusion rates $q_k$
(Section~\ref{sec:pikalman}) and the ladder's coherence-time curves
$L(T)$ cross-check each other and together constitute the first
quantitative decoherence budget for multirotor ego-noise. On bench
motors: $q_k \approx 0$ for $k \le 5$ --- the low comb is fully coherent
over the analysis windows --- and $\tau_k \approx 0.4$--$1.7$\,s for $k =
8$--$40$. On free flight, long-horizon lock at \emph{telemetry-truth}
demodulation is ${\approx}0.1$ at $k = 1$--$2$ and ${\approx}0.03$ at $k
\ge 5$, against 0.24--0.67 on single motors --- the method chain is
validated on the latter, so the collapse is a property of the flight
sound field, not of the estimator. These budgets bound coherent
integration for \emph{any} future method: no estimator that relies on
phase coherence at harmonic $k$ can integrate longer than $\tau_k$,
whatever its architecture.

\subsection{Array counterfactual}
\label{sec:array}

Could the 8-microphone array restore the missing coherence? Two
counterfactuals say no. Plain delay-and-sum beamforming toward the rotor
yields no lock gain. A \emph{self-steered} upper bound --- aligning each
channel's envelope phase to a reference channel using the signal itself
before combining, which upper-bounds every fixed beamformer --- raises
lock at $k = 1$--$2$ only to ${\approx}0.10$. The decoherence is common
across the array at the scales that matter; it is not a
propagation-diversity effect that coherent combination can undo.

\subsection{The displaced comb}
\label{sec:displaced}

The strong low harmonics that \emph{do} survive on DREGON free flight are
not where the mechanical comb predicts: across harmonics $k = 2$--$13$
the acoustic lines sit 0.3--0.5\revps{} \emph{below} $k$ times the
telemetry rate in translating flight. The property was verified by four
independent probes, including a synthetic control in which the estimation
chain recovers an undisplaced comb exactly (exonerating the estimator)
and a hover contrast in which the displacement is 3--4$\times$ weaker ---
implicating translation through the medium rather than instrumentation.
The consequence for evaluation is structural: any refiner that improves
its \emph{audio} lock on these recordings moves its estimate toward the
acoustic comb and therefore \emph{away} from the telemetry reference,
and is charged 0.3--0.5\revps{} by any telemetry-referenced metric.
This, and not estimator failure, is why pi\_kalman is flat on DREGON
steady windows while measurably increasing lock
(Table~\ref{tab:ladder}): near cruise, the audio information is already
priced in. Telemetry-referenced evaluation of audio trackers on
translating flight thus carries an irreducible bias term, and
sub-0.5\revps{} claims on such data should be interpreted against it.

\subsection{Runtime}
\label{sec:runtime}

On a single laptop CPU core the telemetry-refinement configuration runs
at real-time factor 0.037 and the guarded 20\,s blind configuration at
0.35; the dominant costs are the banded envelope solves and full-length
demodulation FFTs, with the time-major banded factorization
5--10$\times$ faster than a general sparse solve and far-pair pruning
removing most demodulation work. The full-coverage blind ladder on the
frozen protocol is heavier --- ${\sim}16\times$ real time per 16\,s
window, dominated by seeding (53--102\,s) and the mid-band DP stages
(205\,s) --- which motivates the neural-seeded hybrid rows of
Table~\ref{tab:scoreboard}: neural seeds replace the blind scan at
${\sim}$real-time total cost. pi\_kalman itself costs seconds per 20\,s
mono cell, comparable to iter\_warp.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the negative result does and does not say}

Section~\ref{sec:negative} is the outcome a reader should weigh most
carefully, so it is worth stating precisely what it licenses. It does
\emph{not} say that the analysis of Sections~\ref{sec:vkrefine}
and~\ref{sec:jitter} is wrong: the VK frequency update is inert under
three independent instruments, the capture arithmetic that explains it is
elementary, and the phase-increment tracker built from that arithmetic is
the only local stage that captures under noise on synthetic data and the
only one whose answer on real bench motors is
initialization-independent. It says that the \emph{assembled} chain, tuned
on synthetic batteries plus two real windows, generalizes to neither real
corpus at the default settings, and that its failure is a capture event of
exactly the kind the capture-range account predicts --- a wide first band
on a residual that still contains a twin's comb. The diagnosis and the
pipeline are, at this stage, on different footings; the paper reports both
at their own footing.

Three practical consequences follow. Widening a band to buy capture range
is not free when the interference is a near-coincident sibling, because
the sibling is inside the widened band by construction --- the $k$-scaled
bandwidth of~\eqref{eq:kscaled} preserves twin discrimination, but only
relative to what the fundamental had, and at $k = 1$ on a twin pair that
is not much. Truth-free acceptance gates recover parity but do so by
declining, which is a way of not being worse rather than a way of being
better. And the single largest cross-drone improvement in this paper came
from correcting the \emph{labels}, not the estimator.

\subsection{What is proven, measured, and assumed}

The claims here are of different epistemic strength and it is worth being
explicit. \emph{Derived exactly:} the demodulation algebra
(Section~\ref{sec:demod}); the phase-noise law and Fisher weights to
first order in noise~\eqref{eq:phasenoise}--\eqref{eq:fisherweight}; the
rank-one-plus-diagonal covariance and its variance
floor~\eqref{eq:rankone}--\eqref{eq:varfloor}, given the arrival-time
jitter model; the banded structure and half-bandwidth of all three normal
equation systems, and the identification of the Kalman/RTS pair with
forward elimination and back substitution
on~\eqref{eq:tridiagsys}; the capture bound~\eqref{eq:captureband} and its
half-width case~\eqref{eq:basin}; the sinc correlation factor and the
$c_{\mathrm{diff}}$ integral under the ideal-band assumption
\eqref{eq:incvar}--\eqref{eq:cdiff}; the two-phasor winding
argument~\eqref{eq:winding}; the parabolic vertex~\eqref{eq:parabola}; and
the Viterbi path's global optimality on its own discretized objective.
\emph{Measured:} every number in Tables~\ref{tab:ladder},
\ref{tab:scoreboard}, \ref{tab:referee}, \ref{tab:floors}
and~\ref{tab:negative}; the three inertness confirmations; the
oracle floors; the telemetry calibration and its controls; the $q_k$
budgets; the array counterfactual; and the displaced-comb property.
\emph{Assumed:} ideal low-pass shapes in both correction factors (real
filters are not brick-wall; the constants shift by $O(10\%)$); Brownian
$b_k$ (the truth may be OU-like or heavier-tailed, in which case $q_k$
measures the \emph{in-band} diffusion only); Gaussianity wherever a
Kalman step is used; and, throughout Section~\ref{sec:jitter}, that
arrival-time jitter is a good model of the shared disturbance --- which is
the assumption S4 exists to test. \emph{Open:} the Cram\'er--Rao
computation from the measured $(\tau_k, \snr_k)$ budgets. The classical
single-tone bound scales as $1/(\snr \cdot T^3)$~\cite{rife1974single};
phase diffusion caps the effective $T$ at $\tau_k$, and assembling the
per-harmonic, per-window bound from the measured budgets is the remaining
piece of a definitive ceiling statement
(\pending{CRB assembled from the measured $(\tau_k,\snr_k)$ budgets}).
Currently the ceiling is established by counterfactual measurement, not by
a matching lower bound.

\subsection{Practical recommendation}

For annotation practice the measured pipeline is: \emph{full-range comb DP
for the global stage, phase-increment tracking (pair-joint) for the local
stage}, with the frequency-update loop removed and any
sibling-subtraction pass behind a truth-free acceptance gate. Run the
coupled VK envelope solve when the reconstructed harmonic waveform itself
is needed (comb subtraction, resynthesis) and as the referee of
Section~\ref{sec:referee}; do not run it as a rate refiner. Where any
per-rotor rate prior is available --- telemetry of any quality, or a
neural regressor's output --- seed with it: neural-trajectory seeding
dominates blind seeding on both pools at a fraction of the cost, and
telemetry-initialized refinement remains the accuracy reference
(0.851 DREGON / 0.784 FLY124 pooled, the latter on the \emph{pre} build). And audit the reference before trusting a
sub-\revps{} comparison: the referee of Section~\ref{sec:referee} costs
one VK solve per candidate constant, and on this corpus it was worth more
than any estimator change we made.

Two live gaps. Ramp windows: the best tracker sits at
2.9--4.0\revps{} against a 0.7--0.9 oracle, a
tracking-under-nonstationarity problem. And blind seeding on a weak fourth
rotor, where the residual re-scan is adjudicating ties of a few percent
of a low-contrast score --- the one place where an outright wrong answer,
rather than an imprecise one, is still routine.

\subsection{Limitations}

The free-flight phase measurements rest on a single cruise window; the
real-data breadth is motors and one flight per rung, not a corpus. The
S4 rung is not measured, so every statement in Section~\ref{sec:jitter}
about $\sigma_J^2$ on \emph{real} data is a prediction. The FLY124
telemetry constants rest on four cruise windows, and the label change and
the audio realignment they induce cannot be separated from each other by
our experiment --- only both-together from the seeding flip; on the one
warm-up window where both-together is a loss ($3.988 \to 5.166$ at an
unchanged seed) we have no explanation. The octave discriminator of
Section~\ref{sec:fullrange} encodes a two-bladed-propeller prior. The
$q_k$ estimates are in-band by construction, so high-$k$ budgets are
lower bounds on diffusion. The displaced-comb consequence cuts both ways:
it exonerates audio trackers on DREGON but also means audio-derived
annotation of translating flight carries a 0.3--0.5\revps{} bias relative
to shaft truth, whose physical origin we have characterized empirically
but not modeled. The method remains non-causal and offline; the twin
ambiguity survives on one of three DREGON recordings in the guarded-blind
configuration; and aggressive maneuvering shrinks every capture margin
involved.

\section{Conclusion}

Blind multi-rotor rate tracking is organized by capture range. Stating a
stage's capture range, resolution and trajectory bandwidth together
explains why a ladder is necessary, predicts which stage will fail on
which data, and --- applied to the classical Vold--Kalman
frequency-update loop --- shows that it cannot operate blind: its capture
radius $B/(2k)$ is $0.13\revps$ where the errors are $0.5$--$1.5$, and
three independent instruments confirm that under blind conditions it does
nothing. This is not a contradiction of the adaptive-VK literature, which
either estimates instantaneous frequency outside the filter or manages the
same radius implicitly by starting wide; it is an argument that the radius
should be stated. We give the replacement --- a phase-increment tracker
whose bandwidth schedule follows from the same arithmetic, and which is
the same banded algebra as the envelope solve it replaces the loop in ---
and we correct the information model that motivates high-harmonic
weighting: shared arrival-time jitter makes per-harmonic errors
correlated, which leaves the weight \emph{shape} intact but installs a
floor $\sigma_J^2$ that no number of harmonics lowers, and makes
$B_k = kB_0$ the natural parameterization. Around the estimator we give
honest floors --- $0.055\revps$ to hold an exact initialization,
${\approx}0.2$ at 0\,dB with four rotors, proved by an oracle that equals
the blind result --- and a label-free referee that both establishes those
floors and, pointed at the reference instead of the estimator, found and
corrected a drifting clock and a $0.7\%$ rate scale in real telemetry.
What we do not yet have is a reconstructed pipeline that beats the blind
baseline on the full protocol: it wins on ramps and loses on steady
windows, for a reason the capture-range account predicts. Reporting that
plainly is the point. The measurements delimit the task --- per-harmonic
coherence budgets of 0.4--1.7\,s, no rescue from coherent array
combination, and an acoustic comb displaced 0.3--0.5\revps{} from the
mechanical one in translating flight --- and within those limits
rotor-speed side information can be manufactured, with stated error bars
and a stated bias, for any recording of a flying machine.
