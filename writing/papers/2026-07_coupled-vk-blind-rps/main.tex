\title{Blind Rotor-Rate Tracking from Multirotor Ego-Noise: Coupled
  Order-Domain Estimation, Phase-Increment Refinement, and the Audio
  Information Ceiling}

\author{Dmitrii~Mukhutdinov%
  \thanks{D.~Mukhutdinov is with the Centre for Intelligent Sensing, Queen
    Mary University of London, London, UK (e-mail: flyingleafe@gmail.com).}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Per-rotor rotational speed (RPS) parameterizes the harmonic structure of
multirotor ego-noise and conditions modern drone-audition systems, yet
synchronized telemetry is rarely recorded. We develop a fully blind
estimator of all four rotor-rate trajectories of a quadrotor from its
acoustic ego-noise, and we characterize the information limits of the task.
The method is organized by a single structural observation: the joint
likelihood over rate trajectories and per-harmonic complex amplitudes is
conditionally linear in the amplitudes --- the Vold--Kalman (VK) envelope
solve is the exact amplitude step --- while the rate block is multimodal at
every scale (octave ambiguities, twin rotors, comb aliases, ramps). The
estimator is therefore a homotopy from a global, discrete, magnitude-domain
comb search (a whitened comb Viterbi extended to full-range ramp tracking
with a blade-passage octave test, an energy-timed takeoff bridge, and trust
gates) to a local, continuous, phase-domain refinement. We derive four
generations of the local step under increasingly honest measurement models,
culminating in a phase-increment maximum-likelihood tracker with a
per-harmonic phase-diffusion model, data-estimated diffusion rates, and a
pair-joint mode for twin rotors justified by a two-phasor winding argument.
A progressive synthetic-to-real validation ladder with per-harmonic lock
and coherence-time metrics, and a frozen full-coverage protocol scored
against raw telemetry, quantify each design choice. Full-coverage blind
pooled error on DREGON free flight improves from 6.80 to
1.81\revps{}; phase-increment refinement improves cross-drone neural
tracks from 1.016 to 0.859\revps. Measured per-harmonic decoherence
budgets, an array-combination counterfactual, and a displaced-comb property
of the sound field in translating flight together bound what any
audio-locked method can achieve against a telemetry reference.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Order tracking, Vold--Kalman filter, drone audition, ego-noise, rotor speed

\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{A}{coustic} sensing from multirotor platforms is dominated by
the platform's own noise. The broadband component of ego-noise is
approachable with conventional enhancement, but the harmonic component ---
combs of tones at integer multiples of each rotor's shaft rate, sweeping
with every control action --- is both the strongest and the most structured
part of the interference. That structure is an opportunity: if the
per-rotor speed trajectories $r_i(t)$ are known, the frequencies of every
harmonic line are known, and both classical projection methods and
rotor-conditioned neural models exploit them to
advantage~\cite{yen2023ncm,gulli2025rotorcond}.

The catch is that $r_i(t)$ is almost never available. Consumer platforms do
not expose synchronized per-rotor telemetry; research datasets either omit
it or record it at rates and alignments unusable for phase-coherent
processing. Of the corpora in common use, DREGON~\cite{strauss2018dregon}
is nearly unique in shipping motor telemetry at all, and even there the
commanded-speed track freezes before landing and must be treated with care.
The practical question is therefore: \emph{can accurate per-rotor speed
trajectories be recovered from the acoustic recording alone, reliably
enough to serve as ground-truth annotation for datasets that lack
telemetry --- and where does the recoverable accuracy saturate?}

This paper answers both halves. The estimator builds on the Vold--Kalman
(VK) order-tracking filter~\cite{vold1993high}, adapted to the multirotor
acoustic setting in the ways summarized below, and is developed under one
organizing thesis (Section~\ref{sec:structure}): every tracker we deploy
--- the comb-matched Viterbi ridge, the coupled VK envelope filter and its
``refine'' step, a Fisher-weighted phase-slope refiner, an
angular-resampling refiner, and a phase-increment Kalman tracker --- is an
approximate maximizer of \emph{one} joint likelihood over two blocks of
unknowns, the rate trajectories $r_i(t)$ and the per-harmonic complex
amplitudes $A_{i,k}(t)$. Given the rates, the amplitude problem is exactly
solvable linear least squares (the VK filter). Given the amplitudes, the
rate problem is violently non-convex, so it splits into a global, discrete,
magnitude-based search and a local, continuous, phase-based refinement.
The refinement variants are four generations of the same conditional
update, distinguished only by the honesty of their measurement models; the
last generation is the only one that survives real flight audio.

The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A frozen full-coverage raw-telemetry protocol} for blind
  rotor-rate evaluation: a pinned dataset, a fixed manifest of 16\,s
  windows tiling entire recordings --- warmup and takeoff ramps included
  --- and per-window permutation-invariant mean absolute error against
  \emph{raw} interpolated telemetry, with any smoothing applied to
  predictions only. Full coverage exposes failure modes that mid-flight
  evaluation segments hide: the previous blind headline was a
  segment-selection artifact (Section~\ref{sec:scoreboard}).
\item \textbf{A ramp-capable blind pipeline} (\emph{fullrange}): a coarse
  full-range frame-rate comb Viterbi with a slope-tolerant transition
  model, a blade-passage-frequency octave test resolving the
  even-harmonic-only warmup regime, an energy-timed takeoff bridge through
  the spool-up masking gap, and two trust gates that revert the coarse path
  to the constant blind seed whenever it is not needed or not trustworthy.
  Pooled full-coverage blind error on DREGON drops from 6.80 to
  1.81\revps{} with steady windows bit-identical to the previous blind
  configuration.
\item \textbf{A phase-validation ladder methodology} (S0--S4) that walks
  from a fully controlled synthetic comb to real free flight in steps that
  each add one nuisance, scored by per-harmonic coherence $C_k$, the
  reference-free lock statistic $\lockk$, an off-comb-corrected variant,
  and a coherence-time curve $L(T)$ --- isolating \emph{which} rung breaks
  each method.
\item \textbf{A phase-increment maximum-likelihood tracker}
  (\emph{pi\_kalman}) whose generative model includes a per-harmonic phase
  random walk with data-estimated diffusion rates $q_k$, whose increment
  variance carries two analytically derived band-limiting factors, and
  which handles twin rotors by a pair-joint two-tone spectral mode
  justified by a winding-number argument. It is the only local stage that
  captures rate offsets under noise, and it improves cross-drone
  neural-seeded tracks from 1.016 to 0.859\revps{} on the frozen protocol.
\item \textbf{An audio-information ceiling for phase-locked refinement on
  free flight}, established by measurement: per-harmonic coherence at
  telemetry truth is nearly absent on free-flight audio; coherent array
  combination cannot restore it; the strong low harmonics that do exist
  are \emph{displaced} 0.3--0.5\revps{} below the mechanical comb in
  translating flight, so any audio-locked refiner is charged this bias by
  a telemetry-referenced metric; and the measured decoherence budgets
  $\tau_k$ bound coherent integration for any future method.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Vold--Kalman order tracking}

The VK filter was introduced for high-slew-rate order extraction in
automotive testing~\cite{vold1993high} and extended shortly after to
simultaneous tracking of orders referenced to multiple independent
shafts~\cite{vold1997multi}; its filter characteristics were analyzed
in~\cite{herlufsen1999characteristics,tuma2000characteristics}. The
second-generation formulation minimizes a data-fidelity term plus a
difference-operator smoothness penalty on complex envelopes, which is the
form we build on. A line of adaptive variants followed: angular-displacement
and angular-velocity reformulations~\cite{pan2007adaptive,pan2010extended},
kernel corrections for the imaginary-part error of incomplete complex
bases~\cite{zhu2023splitcossin}, and ridge-detection-initialized IF
estimation via synchrosqueezing~\cite{wang2020vkifsep}. Closest to our
refinement loop is the iterative adaptive VK filter (IAVKF) of Jiang et
al.~\cite{jiang2024iavkf}, which alternates envelope extraction with an IF
correction computed from the unwrapped envelope phase and adapts the filter
bandwidth through an orthogonality fixed point. Our local stage shares the
same core observation --- the recovered envelope's phase slope \emph{is}
the IF error --- but differs structurally: IAVKF extracts independent
components one at a time by greedy peeling, whereas our components are
constrained to integer combs of four latent fundamentals and are solved
jointly with explicit cross-track coupling. The distinction matters
precisely in our regime, where dozens of harmonics from four
near-coincident combs overlap and greedy subtraction is unreliable.

\subsection{Nonstationary harmonic analysis}

A parallel literature attacks nonstationary harmonic signals by
transform design rather than state estimation. Generalized demodulation
maps a curved instantaneous-frequency trajectory onto a constant
one~\cite{olhede2005generalized}; the fan-chirp transform applies a
common time-warp matched to a harmonically related family of
chirps~\cite{weruaga2007fanchirp}, and the velocity-synchronous linear
chirplet transform adapts the warp to a measured or estimated speed
profile~\cite{guan2019vslct}. Synchrosqueezing sharpens time-frequency
ridges for IF extraction~\cite{daubechies2011synchrosqueezed}, and
chirplet path pursuit performs a global dynamic-programming search over
chirp dictionaries for weak oscillatory
signals~\cite{candes2008chirplet}. Our pipeline contains recognizable
relatives of each: the angular-resampling refiner of
Section~\ref{sec:iterwarp} is a self-referenced fan-chirp/generalized
demodulation, and the global stage of Section~\ref{sec:global} is a
path-pursuit search over comb hypotheses. What the estimation-theoretic
framing adds is an explicit per-measurement noise model --- in particular
the per-harmonic phase diffusion of Section~\ref{sec:pikalman}, which no
fixed transform accounts for.

\subsection{Tacholess and multiharmonic frequency tracking}

Recovering the reference speed without a tachometer is a recognized problem
in machine monitoring; see~\cite{peeters2019tacholess} for a review
and, e.g.,~\cite{wang2019tlot} for a mode-decomposition approach to
instantaneous angular speed estimation. These methods, like ours, extract
speed from the signal itself, but they target a single shaft and typically
operate on vibration signals with favorable SNR. Multiharmonic trackers
with explicit state-space structure go back at least to extended Kalman
formulations of comb tracking~\cite{streit1992multiharmonic}; their capture
behavior --- convergence only within a basin around the true fundamental
--- motivates both the annealing schedules and the ambiguity gating used
here. The harmonic-ambiguity structure we manage coarse-to-fine is the
acoustic analogue of integer-ambiguity resolution in carrier-phase
positioning~\cite{teunissen1995lambda}: low harmonics keep the
``revolution count,'' high harmonics add precision once the count is safe.
In the audio domain, fundamental-frequency estimation is a mature field,
and modern neural estimators are robust in
noise~\cite{han2014pitchnn,riou2023pesto}; polyphonic variants recover
multiple concurrent pitches~\cite{bittner2022basicpitch}. We evaluate
representatives of this family, and stronger drone-specific regressors, as
references on the frozen protocol (Section~\ref{sec:scoreboard}): rotor
combs are not speech- or music-like, the four fundamentals are separated by
fractions of a semitone, and per-frame salience methods do not deliver
sub-\revps{} trajectory accuracy.

\subsection{Drone audition}

Ego-noise suppression on multirotors spans blind source
separation~\cite{wang2020bss}, learned time-frequency
masking~\cite{wang2020dnntf}, and a rapidly growing single-channel deep
enhancement literature~\cite{mukhlish2023survey,liu2025edgebsrof}. Several
recent systems consume rotor-speed side information explicitly: noise
covariance estimation driven by rotor speeds~\cite{yen2023ncm} and
rotor-conditioned enhancement networks~\cite{gulli2025rotorcond} both
report consistent gains from the conditioning signal. This strand is the
consumer of our method's output: high-quality $r_i(t)$ annotation is
exactly what those systems assume and what public data mostly lacks.

\section{Signal Model and Preliminaries}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Generative model}

The data is multichannel audio $x_c(t)$ ($C \le 8$ microphones, 16\,kHz
processing rate) recorded on a quadrotor whose rotor $i$ spins at
$r_i(t)$ revolutions per second --- 74--91\revps{} in cruise for the
platforms studied here. Define the \emph{shaft phase}, the accumulated
rotation angle in radians,
\begin{equation}
  \varphi_i(t) = 2\pi \int_0^t r_i(\tau)\, d\tau ,
  \label{eq:shaftphase}
\end{equation}
so that rate is the derivative of phase. Each rotor emits a harmonic comb:
energy concentrated at integer multiples $k\,r_i(t)$\,Hz of the shaft rate,
for $k$ up to ${\sim}40$ (we cap the analysis at 6\,kHz). The generative
model every tracker in this paper implicitly assumes is
\begin{equation}
  x_c(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{R} \sum_{k=1}^{K}
  \operatorname{Re}\!\left[ A_{c,i,k}(t)\, e^{\,\mathrm{j} k \varphi_i(t)}
  \right] + n_c(t),
  \label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
with $A_{c,i,k}(t)$ slowly varying complex amplitudes --- absorbing the
harmonic magnitudes $a_k$, the initial phases $\psi_k$, and any slow phase
wander --- and $n_c$ collecting broadband aerodynamic noise and any target
sources. The unknowns split into two blocks: the rates $\{r_i\}$
(equivalently the phases $\{\varphi_i\}$) and the amplitudes
$\{A_{c,i,k}\}$. This split dictates the entire architecture
(Section~\ref{sec:structure}). Real audio adds three complications: (i)
broadband noise; (ii) \emph{per-harmonic phase noise} --- the phase of
harmonic $k$ wanders relative to $k\varphi_i$, modeled in
Section~\ref{sec:pikalman} as $\theta_k(t) = k\varphi_i(t) + b_k(t) +
\psi_k$ with $b_k$ a random walk; and (iii) four combs present at once, two
of which form a \emph{twin pair} less than 1\revps{} apart (the DREGON
cruise structure is $\{74.0, 74.7, 85.3, 86.3\}\revps$).

\subsection{Demodulation and the lock statistic}
\label{sec:demod}

Every phase-based method starts by demodulating: multiplying the audio by
a complex exponential built from the current track estimate
$\hat\varphi(t)$ and low-passing. Using $\cos\theta = \tfrac12
(e^{\mathrm{j}\theta} + e^{-\mathrm{j}\theta})$, the $k$-th harmonic
contributes to $x(t)\, e^{-\mathrm{j} k \hat\varphi(t)}$ two terms: one at
frequency ${\approx}\,k(r - \hat r)$, near DC when the track is close, and
an image at ${\approx}\,{-2kr}$, thousands of hertz away. A low-pass
filter of bandwidth $B$ (a few Hz) retains only the first and also rejects
every other harmonic $k' \ne k$, which lands near $(k'-k)\,r$, at least
${\sim}74$\,Hz away. What survives is the complex envelope
\begin{equation}
  z_k(t) = \mathrm{LP}_B\!\big[ x(t)\, e^{-\mathrm{j} k \hat\varphi(t)}
  \big] \approx \tfrac{a_k}{2}\,
  e^{\,\mathrm{j}(k[\varphi(t)-\hat\varphi(t)] + \psi_k)} + n_k(t),
  \label{eq:envelope}
\end{equation}
with $n_k$ the in-band noise, complex Gaussian of total variance
$\sigma^2$. Geometrically $z_k$ is an arrow in the complex plane: exact
track $\Rightarrow$ direction frozen at $\psi_k$; rate error $\delta$
$\Rightarrow$ rotation at $k\delta$\,Hz; per-harmonic phase noise
$\Rightarrow$ the direction random-walks even under a perfect track.

The reference-free diagnostic used throughout is the \emph{lock} statistic
\begin{equation}
  \lockk = \frac{\bigl| \tfrac1T \sum_t z_k(t) \bigr|}
                {\tfrac1T \sum_t |z_k(t)|} \in [0,1] :
  \label{eq:lock}
\end{equation}
the length of the vector sum of the arrows divided by the sum of their
lengths. Full alignment gives 1; random directions give
${\sim}1/\sqrt{N}$, a floor of 0.02--0.05 for our windows. Lock demands
alignment over the \emph{whole} window --- it is a long-horizon coherence
measure. Its measured values anchor the paper's empirical claims: real
single motors on the bench reach $\mathrm{lock}_{10} \approx
0.24$--$0.4$; free flight collapses to ${\approx}\,0.03$--$0.1$ \emph{even
when demodulating with the telemetry-truth phase}
(Section~\ref{sec:budgets}).

\subsection{Phase-noise law and Fisher weights}
\label{sec:fisher}

Two first-order facts about noisy phasors are derived once and reused
everywhere. Write $z = A e^{\mathrm{j}\theta} + n$ with $|n| \ll A$ and
rotate coordinates: $z = e^{\mathrm{j}\theta}(A + \tilde n)$, where
$\tilde n = n e^{-\mathrm{j}\theta}$ has the same statistics as $n$
(circular symmetry). Then $\arg z = \theta + \arg(1 + \tilde n / A)
\approx \theta + \operatorname{Im}(\tilde n)/A$, using $\arg(1+\epsilon)
\approx \operatorname{Im}\epsilon$ for small $\epsilon$. The noise splits
its variance evenly between real and imaginary parts,
$\operatorname{Var}[\operatorname{Im}\tilde n] = \sigma^2/2$, giving the
\emph{phase-noise law}
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{Var}[\arg z_k] \approx \frac{\sigma^2}{2A^2}
  = \frac{1}{2\,\snr_k}.
  \label{eq:phasenoise}
\end{equation}
Second, a rate error $\delta$ read from harmonic $k$ is obtained by
dividing the arrow's angular speed by $2\pi k$ (because $\arg z_k$
advances at $2\pi k\delta$\,rad/s). Dividing a measurement by $k$ divides
its standard deviation by $k$, so the Fisher information about $\delta$
carried by harmonic $k$ scales as
\begin{equation}
  w_k \propto \frac{k^2}{\operatorname{Var}[\arg z_k]}
  \propto k^2\, |z_k|^2 .
  \label{eq:fisherweight}
\end{equation}
This is why every phase method favors high harmonics --- and why
per-harmonic phase noise, which grows as $k$ times any shaft-referenced
wander, is so destructive: it attacks exactly the harmonics that carry the
information.

\section{The Estimation Structure}
\label{sec:structure}

Consider the joint maximum-a-posteriori problem the whole pipeline is
solving:
\begin{equation}
  \max_{\{r_i\},\{A_{c,i,k}\}}
  \log p\big(x \,\big|\, \{r_i\}, \{A\}\big)
  + \log p\big(\{A\}\big) + \log p\big(\{r_i\}\big).
  \label{eq:map}
\end{equation}
Two structural facts dictate everything.

\emph{First, the problem is conditionally linear.} For fixed rates,
model~\eqref{eq:model} is linear in the amplitudes; with Gaussian noise
and a Gaussian smoothness prior, the amplitude block is quadratic and
exactly solvable --- that is the VK envelope solve
(Section~\ref{sec:vksolve}). In estimation-theoretic language the
amplitudes can be marginalized analytically (Rao--Blackwellization):
there is no reason ever to be approximate about the $A$-block.

\emph{Second, the rate block is multimodal at every scale.} Octave doubles
(only even harmonics audible at warmup, Section~\ref{sec:fullrange}), twin
swaps, comb-of-comb aliases, and ramps that leave any local basin. A
gradient or Kalman step converges to the nearest mode; only a discrete
global search over a quantized rate grid is trustworthy about \emph{which}
mode. The pipeline is therefore a homotopy, not an accident:
\begin{quote}
  \emph{global, discrete, magnitude-domain} (immune to phase noise,
  coarse; Section~\ref{sec:global}) $\;\to\;$ \emph{local, continuous,
  phase-domain} (precise within a basin; Section~\ref{sec:local}) $\;\to\;$
  optionally alternate with the exact $A$-solve when the reconstructed
  waveform itself is needed.
\end{quote}
The literal one-solver integration of~\eqref{eq:map} would be a
Rao--Blackwellized particle or mode tracker: discrete hypotheses over
modes, Kalman flow within each, amplitudes marginalized. The two-pass
pipeline is the practical approximation of exactly that object, and the
phase-increment tracker of Section~\ref{sec:pikalman} is its within-mode
flow. The four local-stage variants of Section~\ref{sec:local} are four
generations of the \emph{same} conditional update, distinguished only by
how honestly they model the measurement --- and the frozen-protocol
measurements of Section~\ref{sec:results} adjudicate between them.

\section{Global Stage: Comb-Matched Dynamic Programming}
\label{sec:global}

\subsection{Whitened comb score and Viterbi path}
\label{sec:viterbi}

The first stage ignores phase entirely. Compute a whitened log-magnitude
spectrogram $M(f,t)$ --- whitening divides out the smooth spectral
envelope (running median over frequency) so combs stand out. For a
candidate shaft rate $c$, score the comb hypothesis at frame $t$ by
reading the spectrogram at the comb's tooth positions,
\begin{equation}
  S(c, t) = \sum_{k \le K} w_k\, M(k c,\, t),
  \label{eq:combscore}
\end{equation}
and find the best path $c(t)$ by dynamic programming over a discretized
rate grid:
\begin{equation}
  V(c, t) = S(c, t) + \max_{c'} \big[ V(c', t{-}1) - \gamma\, |c - c'|
  \big],
  \label{eq:viterbi}
\end{equation}
with $\gamma$ a smoothness penalty. By Bellman's principle this recursion
finds the \emph{exact global maximizer} of $\sum_t S(c(t),t) - \gamma
\sum_t |\Delta c(t)|$ over all grid paths --- no initialization, no
basins. That is its superpower: it cannot be trapped by a bad starting
point, and magnitude is robust to phase decoherence because it discards
phase. Its limits are equally structural: precision is capped by the
frequency-bin width and the smoothing prior --- a magnitude peak of width
${\sim}1/T_w$ localizes frequency only to ${\sim}1/(T_w\sqrt{\snr})$ per
window, far worse than coherent phase tracking over the same record ---
and a pair-mean formulation cannot split twin rotors. Measured on the
frozen protocol, the DP ridge attains 0.95--1.15\revps{} per steady
window: the workhorse figure that every refinement stage tries, and on
steady DREGON windows mostly fails, to improve
(Section~\ref{sec:displaced}).

\subsection{Blind seeding: band cap, residual re-scan, stage guard}
\label{sec:seeding}

Blind operation requires seeds for the per-rotor grids. We scan a
whitened time-averaged spectrum with a comb matched filter over a dense
fundamental grid, capping the dictionary at 1.2\,kHz: above that band,
comb aliases of \emph{lower} fundamentals begin to outscore true combs,
and the cap alone repaired the corpus where naive scanning failed
outright. Because a strong rotor can shadow a weaker one at a nearby
fundamental, seeding proceeds in rounds: after each acceptance the seeded
comb is subtracted in the whitened domain and the residual re-scanned.
Finally, the multi-stage refinement ladder that runs downstream is
wrapped in a per-track guard: a stage whose output drops a track's comb
\emph{occupancy} --- the fraction of its harmonics found above the local
noise floor --- is reverted for that track. The guard addresses a failure
mode in which a re-captured track lands on a \emph{louder, wrong} comb;
raw residual confidence increases during exactly this failure, so
occupancy rather than confidence is the reliable signal.

Each component is load-bearing and isolatable. Removing the scan-band cap
returns the cross-drone recording to a 22\revps{} failure (the scan seeds
an alias); removing the residual re-scan leaves its fourth rotor
permanently unseeded (3.24\revps{} pooled --- the guard cannot invent a
missing seed); disabling the stage guard lets late refinement re-capture
one rotor onto a louder neighboring comb (9.7\revps{} on that rotor)
while every internal confidence measure improves.

\subsection{Full-range ramp extension}
\label{sec:fullrange}

The seeder's hypothesis class is \emph{constant} bases estimated from the
time-averaged (cruise-dominated) spectrum, and the tracking DP stages of
the ladder only search $\pm 6\revps$ around them. Two failure modes of
the blind pipeline on full-coverage evaluation live exactly here, and
neither is a filtering problem.

\subsubsection{Octave ambiguity at warmup}
On idle/warmup windows of the cross-drone recording (true shaft
31--41\revps) the spectrum contains \emph{only even} shaft harmonics ---
the blade-passage lines of two-bladed propellers (62.5/72.3/82\,Hz and
multiples), with measured zero odd-line energy. A comb score is then
bimodal in octave: pure comb evidence \emph{cannot} decide whether the
observed line family is the shaft comb or the blade-passage comb of a
shaft one octave down, and the scan's octave-up promotion commits to the
doubled bases (pooled error railing at 33--36\revps). The discriminator is
physical, not spectral: for a candidate base $b$, if the line \emph{at}
$b$ is stronger than the line at $2b$, then $b$ is itself the blade-pass
comb and the shaft is $b/2$ --- at cruise the blade-pass line $2b$
dominates the shaft line $b$. The measured line-strength ratios
$v(b)/v(2b)$ (8192-point whitened time-mean, maxima within
$\pm1.5$\,Hz) are ${\le}\,0.93$ on every cruise window and
${\ge}\,1.87$ on every warmup window; a threshold of 1.4 on the median
over unique seed bases halves all bases (and drops the seed's
gate-calibration, which was fitted on the rejected bases).

\subsubsection{Coarse full-range Viterbi}
To make ramps reachable, a coarse slope-tolerant Viterbi pass is
prepended, producing a time-varying coarse trajectory $c(t)$ over the full
physical range 12--120\revps{} (grid step 0.5\revps; the floor 12
excludes the low-$c$ GCD-alias zone where all $k\le8$ teeth fall into
low-frequency rumble). Critically it runs on a \emph{short-window}
whitened spectrogram at the native frame rate (2048-point FFT at 16\,kHz:
7.8\,Hz bins, 0.128\,s window, 32\,ms hop): window-averaged surfaces
smear a 30\revps-per-second takeoff ramp into invisibility, whereas at
this resolution the $k\le8$ comb sweep is ${\sim}1$ bin per frame per
tooth (an 8192-point FFT smears it over ${\sim}26$ bins). The score is
the \emph{rigid additive union template} $r_0(c) = c + (\text{bases} -
\operatorname{median}(\text{bases}))$ --- all four rotors moved together
--- evaluated with a positive-half-tooth contrast: the mean whitened
value over on-teeth positions $k(c+\text{offset})$ minus the mean
\emph{positive} whitened value over half-teeth $(k-\tfrac12)(c +
\text{offset})$, which penalizes sub-multiple aliases without the
whitening-dip artifact of a signed contrast. Per-frame scores are
soft-normalized (floor 0.3$\times$ the global median contrast) so
weak-evidence ramp frames still express their preference, lightly smoothed
over 3 frames, and chained by~\eqref{eq:viterbi} with $\gamma =
0.4$\revps{} per frame. For windows halved by the octave test the grid is
restricted to $\operatorname{median}(\text{bases}) \pm 16\revps{}$ ---
in the even-harmonics-only regime full-range magnitude evidence is
structurally octave-attracted --- with an adaptive tooth count $k \le
360/c$ (clipped to $[8,24]$) so every candidate is scored on a comparable
band.

\subsubsection{Trust gates}
The coarse DP must not override a good constant seed. Two gates decide,
per window: a \emph{steady} gate --- path span $p_{98} - p_{2} <
8\revps{}$ means there is no ramp to track (steady windows measure
${\le}\,6.5$; the smallest true ramp measures 10.0), so the exact
constant blind init is used, removing coarse wobble on every steady
window; and a \emph{distrust} gate --- $|\operatorname{median}(c(t)) -
\operatorname{median}(\text{bases})| > 5\revps{}$ means the DP abandoned
the seed structure, so fall back to the constant init. The distrust gate
was forced by a measured failure: on two cross-drone maneuver windows the
seeds are asymmetric (a duplicated pair at 74\revps{} plus singles at
82.7/92.35), the rigid template let the DP park the tight pair on the
dominant 91.5\revps{} comb, shifting the path median by $+17\revps{}$ and
turning a 1.18\revps{} window into 15.6; on every well-behaved window the
shift is ${\le}\,1.1$, on the broken ones 16--17.

\subsubsection{Energy-timed takeoff bridge}
Through the middle of a takeoff ramp the narrowband evidence vanishes
under the broadband spool-up: the DP times the low-to-high transition
${\sim}1.5$\,s late, or idles on an alias when an interfering source
buries the idle comb. Acoustic power, however, tracks rotor rate steeply.
When the DP path contains a two-plateau jump exceeding 20\revps{}
\emph{and} the window has a ${\ge}\,1$\,s low-energy idle phase (the
takeoff-from-idle signature; keyed on energy alone the bridge mangled a
maneuver window), the pre-cruise path is rebuilt from the 50--200\,Hz
rotor-rumble band energy profile --- monotone in rate even under the
speech and white-noise interferer recordings, whose 2--6\,kHz band is
flooded. Idle frames receive $c_{\mathrm{lo}}$ from a constant-rate
re-scan of the idle frames restricted to $c \le 0.45\,c_{\mathrm{hi}}$
(excluding the $c_{\mathrm{hi}}/2$ sub-multiple attractor; the DP's own
low plateau is junk exactly when a masker hides the idle comb); transition
frames follow the power law $c_{\mathrm{lo}}
(c_{\mathrm{hi}}/c_{\mathrm{lo}})^{\alpha}$ in the normalized energy
$\alpha$; a catch-up hold at $c_{\mathrm{hi}}$ (the median DP path over
sustained-high-energy frames) persists until the DP path rejoins within
5\revps.

\subsubsection{Ladder init and measured effect}
The ladder initialization anchors on the \emph{seed bases}, not on the
path: $r_0^{(i)}(t) = \text{base}_i + \big(c(t) -
\operatorname{median} c\big)$, clamped at zero --- so any residual
constant DP offset cancels, and gated windows reduce \emph{exactly} to
the constant blind init (steady windows are bit-identical to the
unmodified blind pipeline). The standard refinement ladder runs on top,
unchanged. Table~\ref{tab:fullrange} lists the measured per-window

(Section~\ref{sec:scoreboard}).

\section{Local Stage: One Conditional Update, Four Generations}
\label{sec:local}

\subsection{The coupled VK envelope solve: the exact amplitude step}
\label{sec:vksolve}

Fix the phase tracks $\hat\varphi_i(t)$. Model~\eqref{eq:model} is then
linear in the amplitudes, and the VK filter is nothing more than
penalized linear least squares,
\begin{multline}
  \min_{\{A\}} \sum_t \Big( x(t) - \sum_{i,k}
  \operatorname{Re}\big[A_{i,k}(t)\, e^{\mathrm{j}k\hat\varphi_i(t)}\big]
  \Big)^{\!2} \\
  + \sum_{i,k} \rho_{i,k}^2 \sum_t \big| \Delta^2 A_{i,k}(t) \big|^2 ,
  \label{eq:vk2}
\end{multline}
with $\Delta^2$ the second-difference operator. In the frequency domain a
second-difference penalty acts as a fourth-order low-pass on each
$A_{i,k}$ whose equivalent bandwidth shrinks as $\rho_{i,k}$ grows, so
$\rho_{i,k}$ \emph{is} the per-harmonic analysis bandwidth in disguise,
chosen from a target $-3$\,dB bandwidth by the standard
relation~\cite{tuma2000characteristics}. The objective is quadratic; its
normal equations form a block system whose diagonal blocks are banded and
whose off-diagonal blocks carry the interference terms
$\overline{c_m} c_n$ between carriers $c_m(t) =
e^{\mathrm{j}k_m\hat\varphi_{i_m}(t)}$. Retaining the off-diagonal
coupling is what allows two tracks separated by less than a filter
bandwidth to divide energy correctly instead of both claiming it --- the
``explaining-away'' that makes VK the classical tool for crossing orders,
and that a quadrotor's interlaced combs make mandatory.

Solving~\eqref{eq:vk2} at audio rate for $R \times K \approx 100$ tracks
is unnecessary: the envelopes are narrowband by construction. We
demodulate each track as in~\eqref{eq:envelope} (zero-phase), decimate to
an envelope rate $\fsenv$ of 100--200\,Hz, and solve on the decimated
grid. The decimated problem is not an approximation of convenience:
restricted to envelopes bandlimited below $\fsenv/2$ --- the subspace the
smoothness prior already enforces --- minimizing~\eqref{eq:vk2} at audio
rate and minimizing its decimated counterpart are the same problem, since
every component the demodulating low-pass discards is orthogonal to that
subspace and shifts the objective by a constant. The coupling coefficient
survives decimation exactly for those pairs whose instantaneous
separation falls below $\fsenv/2$ --- precisely the pairs that interfere.
Tracks are partitioned into coupling groups by union--find on this
predicate; each group yields a Hermitian positive-definite \emph{banded}
system under time-major ordering, factorized by banded Cholesky, and
far pairs are pruned with no measurable trajectory effect. The full-rate
signal still anchors each outer round: convergence is monitored by the
audio-rate residual ratio of the full reconstruction, and carriers are
re-derived at audio rate from every updated trajectory. Together these
choices reduce a nominal $10^6$-unknown sparse problem to a sequence of
banded solves (runtime figures in Section~\ref{sec:runtime}).

Note precisely what this solves and what it does not: it is the optimal
\emph{amplitude} step \emph{conditioned on the phase tracks being right}.
The remainder of this section is the history of doing the other
conditional step --- the rate update given the envelopes --- correctly.

\subsection{Generation 1: VK-refine}
\label{sec:vkrefine}

The classical refine step reads rate corrections from the solved
envelopes: if the track is off by $\delta$, each $\hat A_{i,k}(t)$
rotates at $k\delta$\,Hz (the arrow logic of Section~\ref{sec:demod}), so
read the rotation, average over $k$ with weights, nudge $\hat r$, and
iterate; each outer iteration clips the applied correction (default
$0.5\revps$) and re-derives carriers. Fused across a comb tied to one
latent fundamental --- rather than one free IF per component as in
IAVKF~\cite{jiang2024iavkf} --- this is where the rotor constraint adds
noise robustness. Two design choices nonetheless make the step fragile,
and both are now measured facts rather than suspicions.

\subsubsection{Capture radius} The envelope solve only reconstructs
content within its bandwidth $B_k \approx 1.5$\,Hz of the assumed tooth
position. A rate error $\delta$ displaces harmonic $k$ by $k\delta$\,Hz;
once $k\delta \gtrsim B_k/2$ the true energy is simply \emph{outside} the
solved envelope --- the arrow contains noise, and no amount of iteration
recovers. The basin is
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{\max} \approx \frac{B}{2k},
  \label{eq:basin}
\end{equation}
which with $B = 1.5$\,Hz and the informative harmonics $k \ge 6$ is under
$0.13\revps$. Probes confirm: a constant $+0.3\revps$ offset is returned
uncorrected.

\subsubsection{Gate miscalibration} The refine multiplies its correction
by a Wiener-shrink confidence $g = \snr/(1+\snr)$. This is the right
Bayesian instinct with the wrong noise model: on real flight audio the
\emph{long-horizon} envelope SNR is ${\sim}0.03$ --- because of phase
decoherence, not absence of signal --- so $g \approx 0$ and the step is
inert, measured on every real recording including from telemetry-truth
initialization. The information is present at short lags; the gate throws
it away because the model has no concept of ``coherent for half a second,
then re-randomized.''

Annealing the harmonic range (early iterations use a small $K_{\max}$ and
a stiff trajectory prior, widening as the track locks --- the
capture-versus-resolution compromise of extended Kalman comb
trackers~\cite{streit1992multiharmonic}, resolved schedule-wise) widens
the basin~\eqref{eq:basin} but does not repair the gate. So ``VK refine''
$=$ (exact linear amplitude solve) $+$ (a local, narrow-basin,
miscalibrated rate step): keep the first half.

\subsection{Generation 2: Fisher-weighted phase slopes (stage D)}
\label{sec:staged}

Stage D skips the envelope solve: demodulate directly per harmonic with a
fixed $B = 3$\,Hz band, compute per-sample IF errors from the unwrapped
phase derivative,
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\delta}_k(t) = \frac{1}{2\pi k}\,\frac{d}{dt}
  \operatorname{unwrap}\big(\arg z_k(t)\big),
  \label{eq:staged}
\end{equation}
fuse across $k$ and channels with exactly the Fisher
weights~\eqref{eq:fisherweight} under a smoothness-regularized
(pentadiagonal) solve per rotor, clip, iterate. On clean synthetic data
this is nearly optimal and fully captures a 1\revps{} offset. But it has
no per-measurement variance model: the weights assume the phase-noise
law~\eqref{eq:phasenoise} \emph{always} holds, i.e., that $z_k$ is
signal-dominated. Two things break it, both measured on the ladder
(Section~\ref{sec:ladderresults}): any real broadband floor --- it stalls
already at $+10$\,dB SNR, because \texttt{unwrap} commits cycle slips on
noise-dominated samples, and slips are catastrophic unbounded errors that
quadratic weighting does not reject --- and per-harmonic phase diffusion,
whose phase slope is white noise scaled by the diffusion rate,
informative about nothing, yet weighted by $k^2|z_k|^2$ as if it were
pure track error. Stage D is the cautionary tale: right weights, no model
of when the weights lie.

\subsection{Generation 3: angular resampling (iter\_warp)}
\label{sec:iterwarp}

Generalized demodulation~\cite{olhede2005generalized} and angular
resampling attack the capture problem. Change the time variable to
\emph{revolutions of the current track}: define $\theta$ by sampling the
audio at equal increments of $\hat\varphi$ (constant samples per
revolution). Substituting $\varphi = \hat\varphi + 2\pi\varepsilon$, with
$\varepsilon$ the track error in revolutions, harmonic $k$ becomes
\begin{equation}
  \cos\big(k\varphi(t) + \psi_k\big) =
  \cos\big(2\pi k\,\theta + 2\pi k\,\varepsilon(\theta) + \psi_k\big):
  \label{eq:warp}
\end{equation}
in the warped variable it is a tone at exactly \emph{order} $k$ (cycles
per revolution), phase-modulated only by the slowly varying error
$\varepsilon$. A long FFT over the warped window --- the order spectrum
--- turns a nearly constant rate error $\dot\varepsilon$ into a peak
\emph{offset}: the tone appears at order $k(1 + \dot\varepsilon)$, so the
fractional rate error is the offset divided by $k$. This is a
\emph{global search within a band}, immune to the basin
problem~\eqref{eq:basin}, because peak-picking scans the whole search
band rather than following a gradient. Sub-bin accuracy comes from
parabolic interpolation: fitting $p(u) = \alpha u^2 + \beta u + \gamma$
through the log-power values $(a, b, c)$ at bins $-1, 0, +1$ gives
$\alpha = (a + c - 2b)/2$, $\beta = (c - a)/2$, and vertex $u^\ast =
-\beta/(2\alpha)$, i.e.,
\begin{equation}
  u^\ast = \frac{a - c}{2(a - 2b + c)} .
  \label{eq:parabola}
\end{equation}

The refinement proceeds over a ladder of rungs --- the harmonic-ambiguity
schedule made concrete. Round 1 uses low orders (1--6) with long windows
(3\,s) and a wide search band ($\pm2.5\revps$) to keep the revolution
count; subsequent rounds are orders 4--12 / 1.5\,s / $\pm1.8$, 8--24 /
0.75\,s / $\pm1.2$, and 12--40 / 0.375\,s / $\pm0.6$, under the invariant
that the incoming error times the highest order stays below half a cycle
per revolution --- so a peak is never confused with its neighbor.
Measured, iter\_warp is the only pre-Kalman method that captures offsets
under noise ($+1\revps \to 0.04$ at 0\,dB) and the first to achieve
genuine partial lock on real bench motors. Its residual weakness is
within-window stationarity: wandering rates (and wandering twin
separations) smear the long-window peaks --- exactly the regime a proper
state-space treatment handles.

\subsection{Generation 4: the phase-increment tracker (pi\_kalman)}
\label{sec:pikalman}

The phase-increment tracker keeps the demodulation of
Section~\ref{sec:demod} but changes both the measurement and the model.
The measurement is the \emph{phase increment} between consecutive
envelope frames,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\psi_k(t) = \arg\big( z_k(t)\, \overline{z_k(t{-}1)} \big),
  \label{eq:increment}
\end{equation}
the wrapped angle between successive arrows --- computable without ever
unwrapping a long record, so there is no cycle-slip catastrophe: one
increment can at worst be wrong by itself. The model adds what free
flight demonstrably has: the phase of harmonic $k$ is
\begin{equation}
  \theta_k(t) = k\varphi(t) + b_k(t) + \psi_k,
  \label{eq:diffmodel}
\end{equation}
with $b_k$ a Brownian motion of rate $q_k$ (rad$^2$/s; coherence time
$\tau_k \sim 2/q_k$) --- the decoherence channel measured on real rotor
audio (Section~\ref{sec:budgets}). Increments stay informative about the
rate error $\delta r$ long after absolute phase has decohered, because
the diffusion enters the likelihood as \emph{measurement noise} instead
of breaking the signal model:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}[\Delta\psi_k(t)] = 2\pi\,\Delta t\, k\, \delta r(t),
  \label{eq:incmean}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{Var}[\Delta\psi_k(t)] =
  \underbrace{\frac{1 - \operatorname{sinc}(2B\Delta t)}%
  {\snr_k}}_{\text{noise term}}
  + \underbrace{c_{\mathrm{diff}}\, q_k\, \Delta t}_{\text{diffusion
  term}} .
  \label{eq:incvar}
\end{equation}
Both variance factors deserve their derivations; both were load-bearing
bugs before they were fixed.

\subsubsection{Noise term}
The increment is a difference of two phase readings, each of variance
$v = 1/(2\,\snr)$ by~\eqref{eq:phasenoise}. But the two readings share
\emph{filtered} noise: after a low-pass of bandwidth $B$, the noise
autocorrelation at lag $\Delta t$ is $\rho = \operatorname{sinc}(2B\Delta
t)$ (the Fourier transform of a flat band $[-B, B]$ is a sinc). The
variance of a difference of correlated variables is
$\operatorname{Var}[\phi_1 - \phi_2] = 2v(1 - \rho)$, giving the noise
term of~\eqref{eq:incvar}. With $B = 6$\,Hz and $\Delta t = 16$\,ms,
$1 - \operatorname{sinc}(0.192) \approx 0.06$: the increment sees only
6\% of the per-sample noise variance. Omitting this factor overpredicts
the noise ${\sim}16\times$, leads to the conclusion that all residual
variance is already explained, and estimates $q_k = 0$ everywhere ---
precisely the failure of the first implementation.

\subsubsection{Diffusion term}
The Brownian phase passes through the same low-pass before being
differenced. Compute the variance through spectra: a Wiener process has
power spectral density $q/(2\pi f)^2$; differencing over $\Delta t$
multiplies the spectrum by $|1 - e^{-\mathrm{j}2\pi f \Delta t}|^2 = 2(1 -
\cos 2\pi f \Delta t)$; the ideal band keeps $|f| \le B$. Substituting
$u = 2\pi f \Delta t$,
\begin{align}
  \operatorname{Var}
  &= \int_{-B}^{B} \frac{q}{(2\pi f)^2}\, 2\big(1 - \cos 2\pi f \Delta
  t\big)\, df \nonumber\\
  &= q\,\Delta t \cdot \frac{2}{\pi}
  \int_0^{2\pi B \Delta t} \frac{1 - \cos u}{u^2}\, du
  \;=\; q\,\Delta t\; c_{\mathrm{diff}} .
  \label{eq:cdiff}
\end{align}
As $B \to \infty$, $\int_0^\infty (1 - \cos u)/u^2\, du = \pi/2$, so
$c_{\mathrm{diff}} \to 1$ --- the unfiltered Brownian increment variance
$q\Delta t$, as it must. At our $B\Delta t$, $c_{\mathrm{diff}} \approx
0.19$.

\subsubsection{Estimation}
With mean and variance in hand, estimation is textbook. The state is the
rate error $\delta r(t)$, a random walk with process variance
$\sigma_p^2 \Delta t$ per step; each frame supplies one measurement per
surviving (channel, harmonic) pair, with per-measurement variance from
\eqref{eq:incvar} --- the in-band noise floor $\snr_k$ is estimated from
an \emph{off-comb} demodulation at $k\hat\varphi + 2\pi \Delta_{\!f} t$
($\Delta_{\!f} = 3$\,Hz, off every comb line), and frames whose envelope
sits at or below that floor are gated out. The scalar Kalman update is
one line of Gaussian algebra --- posterior precision is the sum of
precisions,
\begin{equation}
  P_{t|t}^{-1} = P_{t|t-1}^{-1} + \sum_{c,k}
  \frac{(2\pi \Delta t\, k)^2}{\operatorname{Var}[\Delta\psi_{c,k}(t)]},
  \label{eq:kalman}
\end{equation}
followed by a standard Rauch--Tung--Striebel backward pass so every
estimate uses the whole window; then $\hat r \mathrel{+}= \delta r$
(clipped) and the next outer iteration re-demodulates. Three ingredients
close the loop.
\emph{(i) Data-estimated $q_k$:} run a first pass with $q = 0$, measure
the robust (median-absolute-deviation) excess of the residual variance of
$\Delta\psi_k$ over the SNR-predicted noise term, and set $q_k =
\max(0, \text{excess})/(c_{\mathrm{diff}}\,\Delta t)$ --- a
method-of-moments estimate of each harmonic's diffusion. On bench motors
this yields the first quantitative decoherence budget of the campaign:
$q \approx 0$ for $k \le 5$ (fully coherent), $\tau_k$ of order 1\,s for
$k = 10$--$40$ (Section~\ref{sec:budgets}).
\emph{(ii) Effective sample size:} the frame rate (62.5\,Hz) oversamples
the band ($2B = 12$\,Hz), so successive measurements are not independent;
the information sum in~\eqref{eq:kalman} is deflated by the factor
$2B\Delta t$ --- the band contains only $2B$ independent complex samples
per second.
\emph{(iii) Ambiguity gating:} an increment is unambiguous only while
$k\,|\delta r|\,\Delta t < \tfrac12$ cycle, so harmonics enter
coarse-to-fine exactly as iter\_warp's rungs --- but the schedule now
\emph{emerges} from a stated invariant plus the measured $\tau_k$, rather
than hand-tuned windows; a wrap guard additionally drops near-$\pi$
increments. Multi-rotor operation refines rotors sequentially inside each
iteration under a per-frame twin rule: measurements of harmonic $k$ of
rotor $i$ are gated out at frames where any harmonic of any other rotor
comes within $B + B_{\mathrm{guard}}$ of it.

Stated approximations: the noise of adjacent increments is treated as
independent (it is MA(1) --- neighbors share one envelope sample); the
wrapped-phase likelihood is treated as Gaussian (fine above the SNR
gate); $q_k$ is time-constant within an iteration. All are absorbed in
practice by the process/measurement variance split.

\subsection{Twin pairs: the winding argument and the pair-joint mode}
\label{sec:pairs}

When rotors $i, j$ sit within the band at harmonic $k$, the envelope is a
two-phasor sum $z = a_1 e^{\mathrm{j}\theta_1} + a_2
e^{\mathrm{j}\theta_2}$. Write
\begin{equation}
  \arg z = \theta_1 + \operatorname{Im}\,
  \log\!\big(1 + \tfrac{a_2}{a_1}\, e^{\mathrm{j}(\theta_2 -
  \theta_1)}\big).
  \label{eq:winding}
\end{equation}
If $a_2 < a_1$, the phasor $1 + \rho e^{\mathrm{j}\beta}$ traces a circle
that does \emph{not} enclose the origin as the beat phase $\beta$
advances, so the log term is periodic with zero net winding: over a beat
cycle, the average rotation rate of $z$ equals $\dot\theta_1$ --- the
increments follow the stronger tone \emph{entirely}, biased by up to half
the split, and no reweighting fixes that. The per-frame twin gate
discards most such measurements, but on tight pairs (split below a
threshold) the collided band contains a usable two-phasor mixture, not
garbage. In \emph{pair-joint} mode, each pair's self-collided harmonics
are demodulated once by the pair-mean phase $k\bar\varphi$ in a band wide
enough for $\pm k \cdot \mathrm{split}/2$; per short window the two-tone
spectrum is fitted (two-peak pick with parabolic
interpolation~\eqref{eq:parabola}, channel-incoherent power) and the two
line frequencies are assigned to rotors \emph{by order} (higher line
$\to$ faster rotor --- immune to search-band ambiguity), yielding direct
per-rotor rate observations that feed the same per-rotor smoothers as
unit-harmonic measurements. The mixture's phase increments are
deliberately \emph{not} used as a pair-mean measurement --- that is the
content of~\eqref{eq:winding}.

\section{Validation Methodology}
\label{sec:validation}

\subsection{The phase-validation ladder}
\label{sec:ladder}

Whether the machinery recovers instantaneous \emph{phase} --- not just
frequency --- is tested on a progressive-difficulty ladder in which each
stage adds one nuisance, so the rung that breaks a method identifies its
failing assumption:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{S0} --- pure additive comb under full control: $1/k$
  harmonic amplitudes on a realistically aggressive synthetic rate
  trajectory at mean 80\revps, gridded over broadband SNR
  $\{\text{clean}, 10, 0, -10\,\mathrm{dB}\}$ $\times$ harmonic jitter
  $\{$none; coherent (shared shaft Ornstein--Uhlenbeck); per-harmonic
  (independent per-harmonic OU calibrated to the $\pm0.6\revps$ IF spread
  observed in DREGON telemetry)$\}$.
\item \textbf{S1} --- a trained neural noise generator, one rotor, with
  its learned per-drone linewidth on; truth shaft phase is the input RPS
  integral, and the generator's own linewidth is part of the measurement.
\item \textbf{S2} --- the generator with four rotors at the DREGON twin
  structure $\{74.0, 74.7, 85.3, 86.3\}\revps$, 8 microphones:
  permutation-aligned metrics plus a twin-capture indicator.
\item \textbf{S3} --- real single-motor bench recordings (8\,ch,
  16\,kHz, setpoints 50--90\revps): no ground-truth phase, so
  recovered-IF stability, lock of the audio against the recovered phase,
  residual harmonic-energy drop under phase-locked comb subtraction, and
  tracker confidence. Initializations: the nominal setpoint and nominal
  $\pm1\revps$.
\item \textbf{S3b} --- real four-motor static recording (all motors at
  nominal 70 on the ground): isolates multi-rotor masking from flight.
  Identical inits are structurally unrefinable for twin-gated methods, so
  a staggered-init cell is the degeneracy-breaking control.
\item \textbf{S3c} --- real quasi-stationary hover (command telemetry
  only): isolates aerodynamic loading and minor motion from translation.
  The S3b$\to$S3c$\to$S4 deltas decompose the sim-to-real collapse into
  masking vs.\ aero-load phase noise vs.\ translation/Doppler.
\item \textbf{S4} --- one 16\,s cruise window of DREGON free flight,
  initialized from tau-aligned command telemetry; IF MAE against the
  measured-speed track is also reported since it exists there.
\end{itemize}
Methods per run: the initialization as-is (the metric floor), stage D,
iter\_warp, pi\_kalman (and its pair-joint variant), VK-refine, and
annealed capture-plus-refine. Synthetic inits are truth plus constant
offsets $\{0, 0.3, 1.0, 2.0\}\revps$. Everything is deterministic (fixed
per-cell seeds).

\subsection{Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

With ground truth (S0--S2): edge-trimmed IF MAE (rev/s); shaft-phase
circular RMSE after fitting the initial phase that maximizes coherence,
plus accumulated phase drift in revolutions; and per-harmonic coherence
\begin{equation}
  C_k = \Big| \tfrac1T \textstyle\sum_t e^{\,\mathrm{j} k
  (\hat\varphi(t) - \varphi(t))} \Big|, \qquad
  k \in \{1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 40\}.
  \label{eq:ck}
\end{equation}
Without ground truth (all stages, so S2$\to$S3 bridges simulation to
reality on the same metric): the lock statistic~\eqref{eq:lock} of the
demodulated envelopes. Two decoherence diagnostics ride on the same
envelopes: (a) an off-comb noise floor --- re-demodulating at
$k\hat\varphi + 2\pi\Delta_{\!f} t$ ($\Delta_{\!f} = 3$\,Hz, off every
comb line) yields $\mathrm{lock}_k^{\mathrm{off}}$, and
$\mathrm{lock}_k^{\mathrm{corr}} = \sqrt{\max(\mathrm{lock}_k^2 -
(\mathrm{lock}_k^{\mathrm{off}})^2,\, 0)}$ is the bias-corrected lock;
(b) a coherence-time curve $L(T)$ --- the mean over disjoint spans of
length $T$ of $|\mathrm{mean}_{\mathrm{span}}\, z| /
\mathrm{mean}|z|$, $T \in \{0.125, \ldots, 8\}$\,s --- which decays with
$T$ at the rate the comb decoheres and separates fast phase noise (short
plateaus) from slow drift (long plateaus).

\subsection{The frozen full-coverage protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

End-to-end trackers are compared on a frozen protocol designed to remove
every degree of evaluator freedom: a content-addressed dataset pin
(\texttt{beatvk-valid-raw@268c766}) covering three DREGON free-flight
recordings (no interferer, speech interferer, white-noise interferer;
8\,mics, 44.1\,kHz resampled to 16\,kHz) and one recording of an
unrelated DJI Matrice~100 (FLY124; 8-mic ring, flight-controller
telemetry at ${\approx}29$\,Hz); a fixed manifest of fifteen 16\,s
windows tiling the recordings \emph{in full} --- each DREGON recording
contributes its takeoff-ramp window plus two cruise windows, FLY124
contributes six windows including warmup spool-up and a maneuver window;
and a fixed scorer: per-window permutation-invariant (PIT) mean absolute
error of the four estimated trajectories against \emph{raw} linearly
interpolated telemetry, pooled across windows. Smoothing arms exist in
the scorer but apply to \emph{predictions only}, never to the reference;
all headline numbers use the unsmoothed arm. Estimator outputs are
stitched over the manifest windows; the scorer edge-clamps outside
covered spans. The ``steady'' subset (DREGON w1/w2 of each recording;
FLY124 w3--w5) excludes each recording's ramp window and FLY124's warmup.
The raw reference matters: DREGON's measured-speed track carries
$\pm0.6\revps$ zero-mean fast jitter, so raw-referenced scores include a
jitter floor that smoothed-reference scores do not --- earlier
guarded-blind results (0.688 pooled DREGON cruise, 1.027 FLY124 cruise on
20\,s windows) were scored against smoothed telemetry and are consistent
with the ${\sim}1.0$ steady-window raw scores reported below.

\emph{First, the previous blind headline was a segment-selection

\subsection{Decoherence budgets}
\label{sec:budgets}

The data-estimated diffusion rates $q_k$
(Section~\ref{sec:pikalman}) and the ladder's coherence-time curves
$L(T)$ cross-check each other and together constitute the first
quantitative decoherence budget for multirotor ego-noise. On bench
motors: $q_k \approx 0$ for $k \le 5$ --- the low comb is fully coherent
over the analysis windows --- and $\tau_k \approx 0.4$--$1.7$\,s for $k =
8$--$40$. On free flight, long-horizon lock at \emph{telemetry-truth}
demodulation is ${\approx}0.1$ at $k = 1$--$2$ and ${\approx}0.03$ at $k
\ge 5$, against 0.24--0.67 on single motors --- the method chain is
validated on the latter, so the collapse is a property of the flight
sound field, not of the estimator. These budgets bound coherent
integration for \emph{any} future method: no estimator that relies on
phase coherence at harmonic $k$ can integrate longer than $\tau_k$,
whatever its architecture.

\subsection{Array counterfactual}
\label{sec:array}

Could the 8-microphone array restore the missing coherence? Two
counterfactuals say no. Plain delay-and-sum beamforming toward the rotor
yields no lock gain. A \emph{self-steered} upper bound --- aligning each
channel's envelope phase to a reference channel using the signal itself
before combining, which upper-bounds every fixed beamformer --- raises
lock at $k = 1$--$2$ only to ${\approx}0.10$. The decoherence is common
across the array at the scales that matter; it is not a
propagation-diversity effect that coherent combination can undo.

\subsection{The displaced comb}
\label{sec:displaced}

The strong low harmonics that \emph{do} survive on DREGON free flight are
not where the mechanical comb predicts: across harmonics $k = 2$--$13$
the acoustic lines sit 0.3--0.5\revps{} \emph{below} $k$ times the
telemetry rate in translating flight. The property was verified by four
independent probes, including a synthetic control in which the estimation
chain recovers an undisplaced comb exactly (exonerating the estimator)
and a hover contrast in which the displacement is 3--4$\times$ weaker ---
implicating translation through the medium rather than instrumentation.
The consequence for evaluation is structural: any refiner that improves
its \emph{audio} lock on these recordings moves its estimate toward the
acoustic comb and therefore \emph{away} from the telemetry reference,
and is charged 0.3--0.5\revps{} by any telemetry-referenced metric.
This, and not estimator failure, is why pi\_kalman is flat on DREGON
steady windows while measurably increasing lock
(Table~\ref{tab:ladder}): near cruise, the audio information is already
priced in. Telemetry-referenced evaluation of audio trackers on
translating flight thus carries an irreducible bias term, and
sub-0.5\revps{} claims on such data should be interpreted against it.

\subsection{Runtime}
\label{sec:runtime}

On a single laptop CPU core the telemetry-refinement configuration runs
at real-time factor 0.037 and the guarded 20\,s blind configuration at
0.35; the dominant costs are the banded envelope solves and full-length
demodulation FFTs, with the time-major banded factorization
5--10$\times$ faster than a general sparse solve and far-pair pruning
removing most demodulation work. The full-coverage blind ladder on the
frozen protocol is heavier --- ${\sim}16\times$ real time per 16\,s
window, dominated by seeding (53--102\,s) and the mid-band DP stages
(205\,s) --- which motivates the neural-seeded hybrid rows of
Table~\ref{tab:scoreboard}: neural seeds replace the blind scan at
${\sim}$real-time total cost. pi\_kalman itself costs seconds per 20\,s
mono cell, comparable to iter\_warp.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What is proven, measured, and assumed}

The ceiling argument of Section~\ref{sec:results} is assembled from
pieces of different epistemic strength, and it is worth being explicit.
\emph{Derived exactly:} the demodulation algebra
(Section~\ref{sec:demod}); the phase-noise law and Fisher weights to
first order in noise~\eqref{eq:phasenoise}--\eqref{eq:fisherweight}; the
sinc correlation factor and the $c_{\mathrm{diff}}$ integral under the
ideal-band assumption \eqref{eq:incvar}--\eqref{eq:cdiff}; the two-phasor
winding argument~\eqref{eq:winding}; the parabolic
vertex~\eqref{eq:parabola}; and the Viterbi path's global optimality on
its own discretized objective. \emph{Measured} (frozen protocol and
ladder; single seeds; one free-flight window for S4): every number in
Tables~\ref{tab:ladder}--\ref{tab:scoreboard}; the ${\sim}0.03$
long-horizon envelope confidence that inertizes VK-refine; the $q_k$
budgets on motors and flight; the array counterfactual; and the
displaced-comb property. \emph{Assumed:} ideal low-pass shapes in both
correction factors (real filters are not brick-wall; the constants shift
by $O(10\%)$); Brownian $b_k$ (the truth may be OU-like or
heavier-tailed, in which case $q_k$ measures the \emph{in-band} diffusion
only, and high-$k$ budgets saturate once decoherence exits the band); and
Gaussianity wherever a Kalman step is used. \emph{Open:} the
Cram\'er--Rao computation from the measured $(\tau_k, \snr_k)$ budgets.
The classical single-tone bound scales as
$1/(\snr \cdot T^3)$~\cite{rife1974single}; phase diffusion caps the
effective $T$ at $\tau_k$, and assembling the per-harmonic,
per-window bound from the measured budgets is the remaining piece of a
definitive ceiling statement --- currently the ceiling is established by
counterfactual measurement, not by a matching lower bound. Mechanism
attribution of the decoherence (masking vs.\ aerodynamic loading vs.\
translation; the S3b/S3c rungs) is likewise ongoing.

\subsection{Practical recommendation}

For annotation practice the measured pipeline is: \emph{full-range comb
DP for the global stage, pi\_kalman (pair-joint) for the local stage};
run the coupled VK envelope solve only when the reconstructed harmonic
waveform itself is needed (comb subtraction, resynthesis), since as a
rate refiner its bolt-on step is inert on real data. Where any per-rotor
rate prior is available --- telemetry of any quality, or a neural
regressor's output --- seed with it: on the frozen protocol,
neural-trajectory seeding dominates blind seeding on both pools at a
fraction of the cost, and telemetry-initialized refinement remains the
accuracy reference (0.851/0.784 pooled). Ramp windows remain the live
gap: the best tracker sits at 2.9--4.0\revps{} against a 0.7--0.9
oracle there, a tracking-under-nonstationarity problem currently being
attacked from the learned side (longer-context regressors), the five
refinement variants having collectively located the information ceiling
near cruise.

\subsection{Limitations}

The free-flight phase measurements rest on a single S4 window (one 16\,s
cruise segment); the ladder's real-data breadth is motors and one flight
per rung, not a corpus. The octave discriminator of
Section~\ref{sec:fullrange} encodes a two-bladed-propeller prior;
three-bladed platforms shift the even-harmonic argument. The $q_k$
estimates are in-band by construction and saturate once decoherence
leaves the analysis band, so high-$k$ budgets are lower bounds on
diffusion. The displaced-comb consequence cuts both ways: it exonerates
audio trackers on DREGON but also means audio-derived annotation of
translating flight carries a 0.3--0.5\revps{} bias relative to shaft
truth, whose physical origin (convective/Doppler geometry of the
dominant sources) we have characterized empirically but not yet
modeled. The method remains non-causal and offline; the twin ambiguity
survives on one of three DREGON recordings in the guarded-blind
configuration; and aggressive maneuvering shrinks every capture margin
involved.

\section{Conclusion}

Order tracking, developed for shafts with tachometers, transfers to
fully blind acoustic annotation of multirotor flight --- provided the
estimator respects the two-block structure of the likelihood. The
amplitude block is exactly solvable and should never be approximated;
the rate block is multimodal and must be attacked globally in magnitude
before locally in phase; and the local step succeeds exactly to the
degree that its measurement model tells the truth about noise,
decoherence, and twin interference. The resulting pipeline --- full-range
comb dynamic programming with physical octave and energy priors,
followed by a phase-increment tracker with a data-estimated per-harmonic
diffusion model --- annotates full recordings, ramps included, at
1.81\revps{} pooled blind on DREGON and refines cross-drone tracks to
0.859\revps, while running on CPU at batch-annotation speeds. Around the
estimator, the measurements delimit the task itself: per-harmonic
coherence budgets of 0.4--1.7\,s at the informative harmonics, no rescue
from coherent array combination, and an acoustic comb displaced
0.3--0.5\revps{} from the mechanical one in translating flight. Within
those limits, rotor-speed side information stops being a dataset rarity
and becomes something that can be manufactured, with stated error bars
and stated bias, for any recording of a flying machine.
